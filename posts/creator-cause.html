Creator Cause

Good morning. How's everybody doing? Anyone live near Kerr County, Texas, or have friends or family there? I'll add them to my prayer list.

As you well know, I try to hold on tight to both the Bible — good hermeneutics — and to math and physics — good scientific method. I need both of them to point to absolute truth. I recently sent around a YouTube video of Alex O'Connor debating AI over what makes more sense about the origin of the universe, based solely on facts. AI concluded, with Alex's help, that the universe requires a Creator. There are only two options: either there's a Creator — a causal power — or there are infinite levels leading up to what we see today, where at each step the causal power is “borrowed” from the step before it. Almost like dominoes. A domino falls — what made it fall? Either someone knocked it over, or there's an infinite line of cascading dominoes each acting on the next. That's called a contingent cause. At that point AI paused and said, essentially, there you have it — a Creator Cause or a Contingent Cause, as if both carry equal merit and it just depends on your viewpoint. But Alex pushed back accurately: if we have this infinite series of contingent objects, each one borrowing causality from before it, with none possessing causal power in themselves, somewhere there has to be an actual causal power to get the whole cascading dependency going in the first place. At that point AI agreed: if you only look at the facts, there has to be a Creator Cause. There must be a God in this sense.

That tracks for me. What I find interesting is that AI wanted to shrug and say “Creator Cause or Contingent Cause, same thing, six of one and half a dozen of the other.” I think that happens a lot in these science-versus-God conversations — it seems to make sense, so you shrug and walk away. But it doesn't actually make sense.

A couple days ago I saw something I'd never seen before. Science is now essentially saying, “you Christians are sort of right — the contingent-cause explanation doesn't hold water, there needs to be a Creator Cause. But you're still mistaken about who that Creator is, because the universe itself is the creator cause.” The claim, from what's being called Quantum AI — the epitome of human intelligence and reasoning capability, apparently — is that “the universe acts as both the creator and the execution of the code.” You Christians can call it God if you want, the argument goes, but really it's just an inherent attribute of the universe to be both causal and contingent at once.

The Quantum AI video

I like Brian Cox — brilliant physicist. Here's what he says introducing that very idea:

“We don't know where the laws of nature came from. We don't know why the universe began in the way that it did — if indeed it had a beginning. So we don't know why the big bang was very, very highly ordered, which is ultimately, as Sean Carroll often points out, the whole difference — the only difference between the past and the future, the so-called arrow of time, is that in the past the universe was really ordered and it's getting more disordered. And that necessary state of order at the start of the universe is really the reason that we exist — because the universe began in a particular form. We don't know why that was.”

What if the universe wasn't built by any external force, but instead coded itself into existence — and what if a Quantum AI just uncovered exactly how? That framing reminds me of a familiar verse:

“Only fools say in their hearts, 'There is no God.' They are corrupt, and their actions are evil; not one of them does good!” — Psalm 14:1

If that's your starting premise, how can you ever arrive at the right conclusion? It's much like how the laws of physics never quite added up when we assumed the universe rotated around the earth. Once Copernicus corrected the premise and showed the planets revolve around the sun — the Son — all the laws of physics fell into alignment.

I'm pretty comfortable sharing my faith in Christ. Being a witness for Christ is easy — being a witness for anything is easy, really. You just say what you witnessed. I have a whole life I can talk about for hours as a witness to the reality of Christ. But I've often struggled with how to get the conversation started naturally — it shouldn't be forced. Christ was the master of this. He made the kingdom of God fit into any context, with anyone, at any time. Once the conversation starts, everything happens organically. It feels right.

So I'd recommend using some of these science-and-AI conversation topics as natural openers with your loved ones. It's a low-pressure starting point — no one's threatened by “did you hear the latest conclusion about God from Quantum AI?” Who doesn't want to talk about that right now? The whole world is fascinated by AI. And what's beautiful is you don't need to defend or argue against AI at all — once the topic is introduced, you just get to be a witness. I wish I was still on campus sharing Christ with international students. — Erv
