Myths and Fairy Tales

Last night I was quoting Mark 4:18–19 and forgot part of the verse — remembered it ten minutes later, but the ship had sailed. I wanted to land on “the desires for other things” — the phrase that hit me harder, years ago, than the bigger “worries of this life” and “deceitfulness of riches” sitting right next to it:

Still others, like seed sown among thorns, hear the word; but the worries of this life, the deceitfulness of wealth and the desires for other things come in and choke the word, making it unfruitful.

— Mark 4:18–19 (NIV)

In our breakout session I shared something I'd read that morning — how C.S. Lewis became a Christian after a late-night conversation with Tolkien about myth, fairy tales, and Christianity:

  • National Review — “How C.S. Lewis Accepted Christianity”

God bless us all. With Himself. Not “other things.” — Erv
