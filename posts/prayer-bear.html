Prayer Bear

Woke up around 4am praying — really more wrestling — over how overwhelming some of the burdens in our BSF group have gotten lately. I don't fully know if I fell back asleep or just drifted into that half-awake place, but a Care Bear started talking to me. I promise I'm not making this up. It quoted Galatians 6:2 at me: “Carry each other's burdens, and in this way you will fulfill the law of Christ.”

My first reaction, once I stopped laughing at myself, was to wonder which translation it was using — NASB or NLT. Either way, I couldn't shake it, so I did what I always do when something rattles around in my head at 4am: I went and chased the actual Greek. “Bear” in Galatians 6:2 is bastazo (G941), and once you start pulling on that thread it shows up all over the New Testament, almost always carrying real weight — literally and otherwise:

  • John the Baptist saying he isn't worthy to carry the sandals of the one coming after him (Matt 3:11)
  • Jesus “bearing” our sicknesses, quoting Isaiah (Matt 8:17)
  • Carrying a yoke that is heavy (Matt 20:12)
  • The disciple sent ahead carrying a pitcher of water (Mark 14:13)
  • The widow's son carried out on a funeral bier (Luke 7:14)
  • “Carry no purse, no bag” (Luke 10:4)
  • The womb that carried Jesus (Luke 11:27)
  • Carrying one's own cross (Luke 14:27)
  • The colt that had never yet carried anyone (Luke 22:10)
  • “I have many more things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now” (John 16:12)
  • Jesus carrying His own cross to Golgotha (John 19:17)
  • Mary asking who had “carried away” the Lord's body (John 20:15)
  • The man carried daily to the temple gate (Acts 3:2)
  • Paul as a chosen instrument to “bear” Christ's name before the Gentiles (Acts 9:15)
  • A yoke our ancestors were not able to bear (Acts 15:10)
  • Paul carried by the soldiers away from the mob (Acts 21:35)
  • The root that bears the branches (Rom 11:18)
  • The strong bearing the weaknesses of those without strength (Rom 15:1)
  • “Whoever it is that is troubling you will bear the penalty” (Gal 5:10)
  • Bearing one another's burdens (Gal 6:2), each carrying their own load (Gal 6:5), and Paul bearing the marks of Jesus on his own body (Gal 6:17)
  • The church at Ephesus that “cannot bear” evil men, yet has borne up and not grown weary (Rev 2:2–3)
  • The beast “carrying” the woman, full of blasphemous names (Rev 17:7)

That's a lot of carrying — sicknesses, crosses, purses, names, penalties, yokes, marks, blasphemies, and each other. It isn't a soft word. Whatever “bearing” someone's burden means in Galatians 6:2, it's the same weight-bearing word Scripture uses for carrying a cross to your own execution.

Hopefully when we meet in a few hours I can connect the dots a little better than a half-asleep 4am Care Bear did. But he wasn't wrong.
