A Model Employee

Happy Friday — killing some time before the 49er/Rams game, and honestly a little nervous the injuries catch up with us. Following up on Tuesday's discussion about Daniel, I've been thinking about three Old Testament figures who all managed the same tricky balance: being genuinely excellent employees to an earthly authority while never losing their first loyalty to God.

Eliezer, Abraham's servant, is easy to overlook, but his handling of finding a wife for Isaac (Genesis 24) is a masterclass in organizational ability, careful negotiation, and faithful obedience to the task he was given. What stands out most to me is how naturally prayer runs through the whole account — he asks God for guidance before he acts, not after things go wrong.

Daniel is the clearer example, and the one Tuesday's discussion centered on. He served Nebuchadnezzar faithfully — genuinely faithfully, by all accounts — while never once compromising his loyalty to God. The dietary law stand in chapter 1, the bold witness to the king throughout the book, the prayer life he refused to hide even under threat of the lions' den, the personal integrity that gave his enemies nothing to accuse him of except his faith itself. He's the model of someone who could serve two masters without ever actually serving two masters.

Nehemiah rounds it out with a different set of skills: vision and planning, delegation, the ability to motivate a demoralized workforce, sharp problem-solving under real opposition, unshakeable integrity, and a dependence on God that shows up constantly in his own prayers woven through the narrative. The wall around Jerusalem, which his enemies assumed would take forever, went up in 52 days.

Put together, the three of them make the same point from different angles: faithfulness to God and excellence in our earthly responsibilities were never meant to be in tension.
