God REmakes.

I believe that the number of words in the Bible’s vocabulary that carry the ‘re-’ prefix is another proof of both its inspiration and its authenticity. Why is that? First let’s look at what the prefix does. In the English language, re- usually means one of three things:

  • Again or anew: rebuild, rewrite, reconsider
  • Back, or to a former state: restore, return, recover
  • In the opposite direction, or undoing: reverse, revoke

Next let’s look at the Bible. In the very first chapters, we see God creating the universe and everything in it — including mankind. But immediately afterwards something breaks. Man sins and rebels against God. He is broken. The woman sins and rebels against God. She is broken. They are covering themselves from each other in shame. They are hiding from God in shame. All their lives they will struggle to scratch a living from the ground — “it will grow thorns and thistles for you” (Genesis 3:18). The world is broken.

But what happens next? What is the first thing God does? What does He continue to do throughout the entire Bible? What culminates in the very last chapter of the Bible? Restoration! From the moment that Adam and Eve sinned and separated themselves from Him, He put a plan in place to solve that problem. Because of their sin the man and the woman were cursed, as well as all of creation — and His plan would solve that as well. Simply put, the entire Bible is about just one thing, and it is stated in the definitions above. It is God doing something again or anew. It is God bringing something, or someone, back to a former state. It is God working in the opposite direction of His curse.

Let’s look at some of these revealing words right now. For each one, I’ll just ask the questions — sit with them; the Bible answers every one.

Reactivate. What did God activate in us again? Our desire for relationship. For connection. With Him and each other.

Reaffirm. What truth or fact did God promise again? That He loves us. Has forgiven us. Wants a perfect relationship with us.

Realign. What direction for us did He reset? His direction — toward Him. Not our direction — toward ourselves.

Reanoint. What blessing did He bestow on us again?

Reassemble. What part of us did He put back together again? What part of the world did He put back together?

Reassure. What certainties did God promise again? What confidences?

Reattach. What did He connect us to again? Himself. Each other.

Reattain. What did He give us again that we lost? Our innocence. Our purity. Our relationship with Him.

Reawaken. What did He wake us to again? How were we sleeping?

Reassess. What about our broken condition was looked at? Discovered?

Rebalance. What parts of our lives were balanced again? How? Why?

Rebirth. What new birth were we given? What type? For what reason? (I wrote a whole post on this one: Born Again.)

Rebloom. What part of us was not thriving or bearing fruit? Where were we dry, or even dead?

Reboard. What did we get back onto? What did we get off of? How did we get back on?

Reboot. Like rebirth — what did we regain so we could begin all over again?

Rebound. How did God give us a second chance? How did He make a way to do better — to get it right?

Rebuild. What did God build again in me? In the world? How? Why?
