Becoming a Mormon

This will be an unusual post. About five months ago some Mormon missionaries came to my door. We talked for a while, and I invited them to come back — which was a little unusual for me. But I've since observed two things from this that might be of value to someone, if not only for me.

1. I've talked a lot with Mormons and Mormon missionaries in my Christian life. In my first year of college, my next-door neighbor in the dorms was a Mormon. We talked every weekend! He was eager to convert me, and I was eager to have an answer for why not — maybe to a fault. I'd only been a Christian a year, but I'd read the entire New Testament at least once. I knew my Bible well enough. However, I most likely fell into that category of “the Lord's bondservant must not be quarrelsome, but kind to all, patient, able to teach…” I got angry. Many of our conversations were intense. Long story short, he eventually did get saved and we went to the same church. Even so, I always felt a little uneasy around him because of an imagined stigma — “See? I was right and you were wrong. Sucks to be you.”

I probably didn't need to write all that, but the point I want to make is that I did not feel that at all with these missionaries. That surprised me. After my frontal lobe damage, I can't regulate myself in any way — if there was ever a time I'd come out swinging, it would be now. Instead, we've been meeting weekly, and I've attended their church once, and even the Temple.

2. This is related to point one, but I was very gracious with them and invited them into my house to talk. I recall reading 2 John 1:10 as a new believer and thinking this kind of hospitality was prohibited. Maybe it is, but in this case I allowed it. I extended that graciousness by listening intently to everything they said and asking good, sincere, honest questions. I also didn't consult AI or my many theological books. I genuinely tried to be objective and present in the moment. Deep down, I sensed I was doing something worthwhile. I was also sharing everything God had done in me, and everything I'd learned and was learning in my daily quiet times — which, for those who know me, is quite a lot! But it came out natural and unforced. They received it well, probably because of how they saw I was receiving their doctrines. Even though I'm more argumentative than I've ever been, I was able to refute errors in their doctrine just by sharing my own personal experience with the verses and ideas they were presenting. The difference from my dorm neighbor was that back then I knew I was being provoked and challenged, and that put me into an entirely different posture — which maybe is why that stigma stuck to me after he got saved.

I don't want this to run too long. Let me add one final piece. I think I'm at a fish-or-cut-bait point with them. One of the senior members of their church met me when I toured the Temple and invited me to dinner tomorrow night. I haven't decided if I'll go — it'll depend on whether my wife agrees. But here's what I sent them and the missionaries just now, because I suspect this will be the focus of their attention going forward. Better to put it out now if that's indeed the case.

Steps to becoming a Mormon

  • Complete an interview with Mormon leaders?
  • Pass an interview on what becoming a Mormon requires?
  • Meet with local leaders — like a final interview?
  • Attend sacrament meetings?
  • Be baptized (again), but in the Mormon church, by…
  • Be confirmed by the priesthood holders in the church

Afterwards

  • Attend church regularly. Accept assignments. Serve.
  • Accept continuing revelation from God through church leadership and additional LDS scriptures
  • Receive a temple endowment?
  • Have my existing marriage sealed
  • No sex, drugs, or rock and roll
  • Be ordained into the Priesthood?

Questions

  • Where/when/how must I agree that God exists as three distinct, separate beings?
  • Where/when/how must I agree that Joseph Smith was a prophet?
  • Where/when/how must I agree to the LDS Restoration narrative?

— Erv
