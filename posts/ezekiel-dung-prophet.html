Ezekiel, the Dung Prophet

Brothers, have you ever looked at word clouds for individual books of the Bible? I love them — they show which words show up most often in a given book, which is a fast way to get a feel for what that book is really about. I recently noticed that Ezekiel uses the word dung constantly, almost always in reference to the idols of Israel.

I remember being amazed the first time I read that God told Ezekiel to cook his food over human dung — and not for one meal, but for 390 days. That got me digging into just how much dung shows up in Ezekiel, beyond the obvious. Ezekiel talks a lot about God's judgment on Israel for worshiping idols, judgment that eventually lands them in Babylonian captivity. But of all the idols the Bible could single out, Ezekiel zeroes in on one specific kind: the dung idol.

It made me think of Jeremiah, Ezekiel's contemporary, known as the “Weeping Prophet” because he was so grieved by his people's persistent sin and refusal to repent. I actually admire that title — I feel that same grief over the state of the USA sometimes. But to go down in history as the “Dung Prophet”? That's a rough draw. Picture the two of them at a retreat, forced to introduce themselves. Jeremiah stands and says, “My name is Jeremiah. I'm a prophet — people call me the Weeping Prophet, because of the tears on my face when I speak to my people.” Ezekiel is crouched down hoping no one calls on him next. They do. He stands: “My name is Ezekiel. I'm also a prophet. People call me the Dung Prophet — because of my message of dung to the people.”

For fun: do you know what the single most frequent word in the Bible is, excluding conjunctions, prepositions, and proper nouns? It's “One.” That surprised me at first, but the longer I sat with it, the more it seemed like the actual central message of the whole Bible, in more ways than I could count. Might make a good Fellowship Night activity — everyone goes around and shares one way “One” is a central theme. I came up with roughly ten myself. — Erv

On the dung front specifically: the Hebrew word gillulim (גִּלּוּלִים) appears close to 48 times in the Old Testament as a contemptuous term for idols — and about 39 of those occurrences are in Ezekiel alone. Its literal root means “round things,” referencing dung pellets, and in context it's best understood as “dungy idols” or “crap gods” — a deliberate equation of false-god worship with worthless excrement. It shows up across Leviticus, Deuteronomy, 1 and 2 Kings, and Jeremiah, but Ezekiel returns to it again and again — chapters 6, 8, 14, 16, 18, 20, 22, 23, 30, 33, 36, 37, and 44 all use it, usually tied directly to a specific judgment for idol worship.

In short, gillulim is a term of pure derision — God, through Ezekiel, calling Israel's idols exactly what they are: worthless, dungy, and unworthy of the devotion poured out on them.
