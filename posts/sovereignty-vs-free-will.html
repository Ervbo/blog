Biblical February 18, 2027

Sovereignty vs. Free Will

Thanks for another great night of sharing, brothers — even with a loose cannon like me in the room. I'll confess I went home and had a coffee cup of rum afterward, just to process it all.

Random tangent first: if you haven't seen the video of an ant walking on a piece of paper, it's oddly mesmerizing — and it reminded me of a Spurgeon quote I keep coming back to: “There is enough corruption in the best man to damn him as certainly as the grossest sin that ever was committed.” Also, did you know horns show up as a symbol of strength throughout the Old Testament (Ps 92:10)? Neither did I, until recently.

But the real thread from our discussion was suffering. I keep asking myself: what would Jesus mean to you if He had never suffered? Luke 24:26, Mark 8:31, Acts 17:3, Hebrews 2:10, and 1 Peter 1:11 all point to the same thing — His suffering wasn't incidental to who He is, it's central to it. C.S. Lewis's The Problem of Pain and Philip Yancey's Where Is God When It Hurts both wrestle with this better than I can. Some of the reasons Scripture gives for suffering: our own sin, the sinfulness of the world we live in, the work of Satan, sanctification, persecution (all over Acts), being an example to others, and the cosmic battle playing out behind the scenes. Hebrews 5:8 says Christ Himself learned obedience through what He suffered. Colossians 1:24 goes further — Paul talks about filling up in his own body what's still lacking in Christ's afflictions.

And evil wins. A lot. Daniel 7:19–21, 23, 25 and Daniel 8:4, 10–11, 13, 19, 23–25 both describe a beast that genuinely prevails against God's people for a time. Revelation 12:7–8, 10, 17 shows the same pattern playing out on a cosmic scale — real losses, real ground given up, before the final outcome.

On a lighter note — how do you tell a dream is from God versus just your own overactive imagination? Scripture gives us plenty of examples either way: Genesis 20:3, 28:12–15, and 41:25–32; Matthew 1:20–21; Daniel 2:1–49. My own theory, only half-joking, is that spicy food might be a contributing factor on the “just your own thoughts” side of the ledger (Eccl 5:3, 7; Jer 23:25–32).

From this week's lesson: how will you pray for those who are persecuted for their faith (Luke 22:31–32; Heb 13:3)? And on a completely unrelated note — Bible Clock! Time to order one, if you haven't already.

God bless you guys!! — Erv
