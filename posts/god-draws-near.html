God Draws Near

I like how a new thought about God always pops up in my head. It’s hard to say where they come from. I know Jesus promised us that the Holy Spirit would bring to remembrance all the things that Jesus said. But I think it is more than that. In the Old Testament it is stated clearly and repeatedly how important it is to not only read God’s Word but to memorize and meditate on it. I have done that pretty much my entire life, but more intently the first ~10 years of being saved. I am sure that gives the Holy Spirit more to work with even today. I am also always not only in God’s Word but in many things related to God’s Word: Bible Study Fellowship (BSF), church book club, Bible apps, Bible resources, sermons on YouTube, devotionals, and my leisurely reading. Those provide even more opportunities for the Holy Spirit to get my attention. But why am I saying all of this? Because my thoughts for today seem to come out of the blue. Usually I can trace them to something in the list above. Not this time.

I want to talk about God drawing near to us — what it looks like in the Bible from Genesis to Revelation. I can see it in my head. In many ways it is so beautiful. In others, as we will see, it is extremely sad. But before I talk about the beauty and the ugly of it, I want to take this opportunity to mention what I believe the overall message of the Bible is. Have you been asked that before? I sure have. And it needs a good answer! Because many will not read the Bible for themselves to find out. They will listen to our summary, and they will read the Bible by watching how we live — but for some reason they will avoid picking up the book at all. My summary of the Bible is this:

A lovesick Father desperately looking for His children to bring them home.

The beauty of God drawing near to us has so many levels. I won’t pretend to know them all, or even explain them as they deserve. But what I will say is this. The first beautiful thing I see is that God wants to be near to us! And that is the understatement of the century! It reminds me of that verse in Isaiah — can a mother forget her nursing child? Neither can I forget you. Does a mother want to be close to her child at any age? Yes! The bond between a mother and her child is unmatched. It is obvious for all to see. There are many reasons for it — one fascinating reason is the physical connection in the blood between the two that remains long after birth. Look up microchimerism for more information.

The next thing I see is that He still wants to be near to us after we disobey Him and choose to go our own way without Him! He knew Adam and Eve had sinned when He came looking for them. He knew where they were hiding when He asked — where are you? He knew why they knew they were naked. And in love, the first thing He does is address their shame (nakedness) and put forth a plan to redeem them. He explains it clearly to Adam, Eve, and the Serpent. He also shows great love, even though it may not be obvious, by sending them out of the Garden. It’s not a vindictive or angry “You’re out! You don’t belong here. You don’t deserve all this beauty I have given you.” No. What He is really saying is — You have sinned. You are now unholy. I am holy. Therefore I can never be close to you in this condition. If you were to remain in the Garden and eat of the Tree of Life, you would be eternally separated from Me.

The next thing I see is the unfolding of this plan — to not only remain close to us but eventually solve the problem once and for all. God appears to Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and Joseph, creating a new line of people that will be His chosen people. He will provide them ways to be close to Him by addressing their sin with offerings and sacrifices. His chosen people will carry this message to the entire world and eventually provide the Savior of the world, which is His final solution. When they are in captivity He delivers them so He can be with them (Exodus 3:18, 5:1–3, 8:27).

I am going to pause here — blogs are not to be too long, and I have a lot more to say. Below is my summary of the whole story, from the Garden to the New Creation. I will finish this post in Part 2.

The Bible can almost be read as the story of God moving closer to humanity, humanity withdrawing through sin, and God making a way back.

The Story of God’s Presence with His People
