Dance with the Spirit.

I met with my pastor recently and it was a wonderful time. We were talking about revival — the kind that shows up here and there across church history, the kind I got a small taste of myself in the Jesus Movement, under Chuck Smith at Calvary Chapel, back in high school. It got me wondering: are there examples of revival in the Bible itself? And if there are, is there any pattern to what precedes one, or is it purely at God's initiative, on His own timing?

In that same conversation, something my pastor said triggered a chain of thought about the language the New Testament uses for the Christian life. Walk by the Spirit. Live by the Spirit. Be filled with the Spirit. Be led by the Spirit. I've heard preachers expand “walk” into “walk in lockstep with” the Spirit, and that's when another word came to mind: dance. David danced before the Lord with all his might in 2 Samuel 6:14 — unselfconsciously, fully.

I ran the idea past a language question before running with it too far: does the Greek behind “walk by the Spirit” (peripateō, in Galatians 5:16) carry any of that? Strictly, no — Greek has its own separate words for dancing (choreuō, orcheomai), and peripateō just means to conduct one's life, a common Jewish idiom for walking in God's ways. But Paul doesn't stop at “walk.” In Galatians 5:25 he switches to stoicheō — “keep in step with the Spirit” — a word that evokes marching in formation, coordinated movement, rhythm. That's closer to what I was reaching for.

So linguistically, “walk” doesn't mean “dance.” But theologically, the metaphor holds up better than I expected. A dance has a leader and a follower, a rhythm, a responsiveness. The Spirit leads; we follow. There's grace in it, and surrender, and trust — which happen to be exactly the qualities Paul is calling for when he tells us to keep in step. We're the bride of Christ, after all, which only strengthens the picture.

It also explains something else about me: I'm a genuinely terrible dancer in real life. Which tracks — because I'm apparently just as clumsy at the spiritual version.
