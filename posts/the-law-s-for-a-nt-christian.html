The Law(s). For a NT Christian.

Sometimes my brain has a mind of its own — a downside, maybe, of reading the Bible enough that it never really turns off, even at 4am. I woke up recently, ostensibly to use the bathroom, but it was really a trick: once I was awake, my brain wanted to know whether a Christian should obey the Ten Commandments. Should we obey all the Old Testament Laws? So there I was, sitting in the living room, trying to answer myself.

My first move was to split the question, because “the Law” isn't really one thing — it's Moral, Civil, and Levitical, and each one gets a different answer.

Levitical. These were always shadows pointing to Christ. He fulfilled all of them. We can learn a great deal from them, but we're no longer under them.

Civil, or social. Generally, yes — and if I'm not mistaken, most of the foundational laws in the US actually trace back to these. I can't think of a good reason we shouldn't still keep them.

Moral. This is really the Ten Commandments, and the easy answer is: absolutely, all of them. But then someone asks — so we should keep the Sabbath? Yes, and more. I'm firmly in the camp of interpreting the Bible with the Bible — reading the Old Testament in light of the New. And what does the New Testament consistently do with these commands? It intensifies them. Don't commit adultery becomes don't even look with lust in your heart. Don't murder becomes don't even hold anger toward your brother. Keep the Sabbath becomes keep every day, every moment, holy to the Lord.

That idea of more is what's been running through my head since. There are dozens of New Testament passages that cite an Old Testament example and then push further — how much more should we, who are dead to sin, no longer live in it; how much worse it is to neglect so great a salvation. I count at least ten of these “how much more” statements addressed to New Testament believers. Tracking those down properly is my homework for today — along with untangling the very different purposes behind these three categories of law in the first place, which really needs addressing before any of these questions get a fuller answer.

I guess that's it. Maybe I can go back to sleep now.
