God’s negative attributes

Several of God's real attributes feel “negative” to us mostly because the only versions we've ever encountered are the corrupted, human ones. In Scripture, though, these traits are holy, loving, and held in perfect balance with all of His other perfections. Here's what stood out to me digging into this:

Severity. Paul tells believers in Romans 11:22 to “consider the kindness and the severity of God.” Human severity is usually harshness. God's severity is righteous judgment — proportionate to real evil, never cruel or out of control, and it's actually what protects the world from unrestrained wickedness.

Wrath and vengeance. Nahum calls Him “a jealous and avenging God,” with wrath aimed at His enemies and the unrepentant. Human vengeance is petty or selfish. God's is the execution of perfect justice, rooted in holiness and love for what's good — no injustice, no excess, no bad timing.

Consuming fire. Deuteronomy 4:24 and Hebrews 12:29 both say it: “The Lord your God is a consuming fire.” Human burning anger is dangerous. God as consuming fire means His holy presence burns away idolatry and evil — terrifying to those in rebellion, but genuinely comforting to anyone who longs for a world cleansed of injustice.

Holy jealousy. Exodus 20 and 34 present God's jealousy as intense, covenant love that refuses to share His people with idols. Our jealousy is usually insecurity or envy. His is the opposite — He lacks nothing. He's jealous because divided loyalty destroys His people and denies Him the glory that's rightly His.

Anger. Scripture speaks of the Lord's anger against sin in the same breath as calling Him “merciful and gracious, slow to anger, abounding in steadfast love” (Exodus 34:6–7). Human anger is rash and self-interested. His is measured, holy, and never at odds with His mercy.

Every one of these “hard” attributes — severity, wrath, consuming fire, jealousy, anger — sounds frightening when we picture human passion behind it. In God, they're pure expressions of holy love and justice, without a trace of the distortion or instability that make those same traits sinful in us.
