The Giving Tree. Part 2.

I am back, and am happy to finish the post I started last week. I could have easily finished it then, but I am trying to be sensitive to those who read my posts. I want to honor your time and your intellect. I will always put effort into keeping these posts interesting and relevant but also as succinct as I can. Also, I was getting a little too emotional at the end.

I had been talking about love. The various definitions of it in the Greek. The overall sense of each word and an example. I then went deeper into the examples of how people express their love and what the underlying conditions are. All with the focus being on ‘unconditional’ love being the greatest. That when the Bible states that “God is love” it is saying He is ‘unconditional’ love.

I then touched on the fact that ‘unconditional’ love existed before Christ. We see it in mothers and celebrate it. We see it in dogs and celebrate it. I don’t mean to cast shade on mothers or dogs, but even their love can’t compare to that of God’s love in Christ. His love is infinitely deep and eternally present. I chose just one example from His life to highlight this fact. When He prayed, “Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing.” This one verse alone reveals so much about Christ and His heart. Just how great His love is for us. In a very real sense we did every evil thing imaginable to Him. I can’t even think of anything worse that could have been done. How could we have hurt Him even more?

But let’s pause and take a moment here. Has anyone ever deliberately hurt you? Physically? Sexually? Mentally? Emotionally? Has anyone not only broken your heart but crushed it? Has anyone ever deliberately looked for ways that they could hurt you even deeper? That they are not satisfied that you have suffered enough? What was your response? They have never considered your pain. Never even considered that they should ask for your forgiveness. Did you forgive them even though they did not ask for it? Did you pray that God would forgive them? Or did you think — how can I hurt them in return? Did you ever get to a point where you thought — I HATE THEM! I want to kill them! Jesus had every right to feel that way. You do realize that, don’t you? Except for one thing. He loves us. Unconditionally. And when you truly love someone this way there is nothing they can do, no matter how evil, how disgusting, to diminish that love in any way. Not even an iota.

The Giving Tree

Here is where I want to talk about The Giving Tree. I am sure you have heard of it. It might be one of the most popular children’s books of all time — published back in 1964 — though I think it received only one notable recognition, a New York Times Outstanding Book selection in 1974. And it has become one of the most divisive books in children’s literature. Shel Silverstein was a genius before his time, in my opinion. The book is simply about an apple tree and a boy who develop a relationship with each other. The tree is very ‘giving’ and as the boy ages he becomes more ‘taking.’ Without going into the details, the book basically ends with the tree having nothing left to ‘give’ and the boy, now a man, with nothing left to ‘take.’ Which becomes a two-edged sword. The root of the division that will arise. Was this a healthy relationship? Does the tree love too much? Was the tree codependent? Does the boy reveal that humanity will always abuse unconditional love because it is selfish at its core?

The conflict is easy to see. Selfishness vs. codependency. A codependent relationship is an unhealthy dynamic in which one person consistently prioritizes the needs of another over their own, often losing their sense of self while enabling the other’s behavior. Conversely, a selfish relationship is also an unhealthy dynamic — one in which a person consistently prioritizes their own needs over others. Enlarging themselves at the expense of others.

What do you think? Where do you stand? Consider this. Is Jesus like the tree? Does He love us? Does He give all He can until He can give no more? Are we like the boy? Do we love Jesus? Or do we take all that we can from Him until we can take no more?

We all need to pray for wisdom about this. Because what you believe about this book and where you stand in this conflict will translate into how you live your Christian life. I know it has in mine!

My thoughts

In codependency, the person who consistently prioritizes the needs of others over their own does so because they have a poor view of themselves. They have little to no self-worth. They do not love themselves. They can’t handle being alone. They ‘need’ the other person desperately. That is why they go to these extremes. It is not even a choice for them. Their entire life exists only if that other person loves them and stays with them. The other person becomes the only reason for them to live. Finally, I must wonder if the person who is constantly giving to their own demise is truly happy. They are happy in some way. I can see that. That the person they can’t live without is still around. But that can’t be real happiness. Where real happiness is someone who stays with you because they also love you and reciprocate.

Let’s apply this to Christ. Does Jesus have a poor view of Himself? Not possible. Does He have no self-worth? Not possible. Does He need us, or anyone, to love Him? Absolutely not. Does He want us to love Him? Immeasurably. Does He choose to give us all He can? Absolutely. He does not have to for any other reason than His love. Does He exist only if we love Him? No. Is He reduced in any way if we leave Him? No. Is He truly happy when He gives to us sacrificially, to His own hurt? Yes! But not in ‘some’ way. Not because we stick around. He is truly happy because ‘unconditional’ love is not affected by the response of the one loved. It is happy in and of itself in the giving.

One last difference between the picture and the reality. The book ends with a stump — a tree with nothing left to give. But Jesus is not a stump. He gave everything, down to His last breath — and then He rose, with infinitely more to give. The tree’s story ends spent. His never will.
