Satan. Smarter than a Third Grader?

A thought has been circling my head for a while, taking different shapes but always coming back to the same core idea: the ongoing chess match between Satan and God. His war with mankind, I understand well enough — he hates everything of God, so it's only logical he'd try to destroy us, if only to wound God through us. Unfortunately, that's a fairly easy task for him. I've always loved Paul's line in 1 Corinthians that we're “not ignorant of the schemes” of the devil — the Greek word there really means “methods.” And 1 John spells the methods out plainly: the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the boastful pride of life. That's it. We see the same formula on Eve in Genesis, and it hasn't changed since.

But taking down God is a different story entirely. We've talked before about how Satan's real opposite isn't God — it's Michael the Archangel. There's nothing and no one like God. He has no equal, no opposite. Angels are powerful and intelligent to a degree; God is omnipotent and omniscient, full stop. You do not want to play chess against Him.

Which brings me to the thought itself. Mankind was clearly confused about who Christ was while He was here — it's almost entertaining reading the range of opinions people had about Him, especially as that confusion peaked at His death and resurrection, even though He'd told them plainly, repeatedly, what was coming. But if man wasn't that sharp about it, I don't think Satan was either. If he had been, he wouldn't have pushed Judas to betray Christ, or pushed the Jewish leaders to demand His crucifixion. That might be the single dumbest move he ever made. I don't know exactly what happened during the three days Christ was “in the grave,” because He wasn't really in the grave — Scripture tells us He was in Sheol with Satan, taking the keys and setting the captives free. I have to believe Satan was genuinely shocked to see Him there.

It all traces back to the very first move on the board, in Genesis: “You will bruise Him on the heel, He will bruise you on the head.” Which, in the end, might be the most brilliant chess move ever played — and Satan walked straight into it, more than a thousand years of prophecy notwithstanding.

Not sure I'm exactly right here, but if I am, it would explain a lot — why mankind was so confused, and why Satan was defeated so completely. And if I'm wrong, it was still fun to think through.
