Blessing(s).

Are you like me, and have a hard time wrapping my head around many things in the Bible? Words, verses, actions (by God and others), thoughts, ideas, parables, theophanies, individuals, and certain prophecies? If so, let’s be friends. I want to talk here about a word used a lot throughout the Bible: the word ‘bless’ and its word family, which is used 519 times (thank you, AI). As much as I would enjoy researching all 519 occurrences and sharing the interesting nuances with you, I think I would add more value by just sharing what I know through my life experience. I am now 68 years old; I have a lot of that now. You might have to run this blog post through an AI tool with the cynical-and-jaded filter turned on. J/K! I fully expect this to be a blog with many ‘chapters.’ But let’s get into it and see how far we can get now.

Like with pretty much everything in life, it is always best to start with a good definition. So many problems in life and in communication can be avoided or minimized just by doing that. I recall, after years at Intel and giving millions of presentations, I noticed a trend: pretty much every slide in my presentation stated something or showed something, and then I simply ‘defined’ it further, and then answered questions.

Let’s start with Blessing

A blessing that is referred to in the Bible can be, and usually is, a good thing that God gives. Already we need to pause. Let’s talk about ‘can be’ and ‘good thing.’ ‘Can be’ is a qualifier for us as humans, based on what we think is a ‘good thing.’ From God’s dictionary, a blessing is always a ‘good’ thing. Because He is good — all that He is defines good — it is impossible for it to be any different. And tying it to His immutability, it will always be just that. But for us it isn’t so clear. If it hurts us in any way, if it is negative or even has the slightest negative effect, we are first to reject the idea that it could be a blessing in any way. And to be fair, I think that is okay. To a degree. Remember the famous chapter in Deuteronomy? Chapter 28. It is God’s pronouncement on what a blessing or a curse will look like depending on how we respond to Him. If you look down the list, all the good things are called blessings and all the bad things are called curses. So, it is okay to start there. But as we all know, correlation is key in Bible study, and there are many places where it goes beyond that. I won’t go into it right here, but just by contrasting Deuteronomy 28 with the entire NT, you can see that all the bad things we see there are not curses by God. A simple look at all that Jesus endured throughout His ministry — and similarly what His disciples went through — shows the opposite! They are signs of blessing and obedience. But let’s table that for now.

Back to our definition. The blessing we see throughout the Bible can be a declaration or a prayer invoking the ‘good,’ or simply recognizing a ‘good’ thing or ‘tangible benefit’ in someone’s life. Many times it is simply a synonym for giving praise to God — “Bless the LORD, O my soul!” (Psalm 103:1). We can’t go much further in this regard, as it depends on the context in the Bible where we encounter it. Again, just knowing whether the word is occurring in the OT or the NT is needed.

The landscape of the blessing

Before we dig into the original languages (that is coming in the next chapter), here is the whole territory at a glance — every direction a blessing travels in the Bible:

DirectionWhat ‘blessing’ ordinarily means in that relationshipRepresentative passages
God blesses peopleGod grants good, favor, fruitfulness, protection, provision, or covenant benefits.Genesis 1:28; 12:2–3; Numbers 6:24–27
People bless GodPeople praise, thank, and acknowledge God; they do not make God more prosperous or complete.Genesis 14:20; Psalm 103:1–2
People bless other people with wordsThey invoke God’s favor, pronounce a benediction, express goodwill, or recognize a person’s blessed condition.Genesis 14:19; 48:15–20; Ruth 2:4
People bless others through gifts and actionsThey give a tangible benefit, act generously, or become a source of good.Genesis 33:11; 1 Samuel 25:27
Scripture calls someone blessedIt identifies a favorable or enviable condition — not necessarily a pleasant emotional state.Psalm 1:1; Matthew 5:3–12

That is chapter one: the definition and the map. In the next chapter we will meet the word family itself — bārak, bərākhāh, and ’ashrê in the Hebrew; eulogeō, eulogia, and makarios in the Greek — and find out what 519 occurrences have to teach a 68-year-old who thought he had a lot of life experience. See you there.
