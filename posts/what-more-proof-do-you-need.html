What More Proof Do You Need?

Brothers, good morning! What must that resurrection morning have been like when Jesus rose from the dead — all of humanity hanging in the balance! I don't know which was more pivotal, God becoming a man and stepping into our world, or His defeat of sin and death before He left. I'm going to have to go with “both.”

I was thinking last night about what it actually takes for a person to believe in something — anything. I even ran this exact question by Perplexity to see what it would say.

To me, I believe in God for a mountain of reasons. Science alone proves His existence beyond a doubt, in my mind. I love Sir Francis Bacon's line: “A little knowledge of science makes man an atheist, but an in-depth study of science makes him a believer in God.” And that's before you even get into the other ten-plus categories our legal system recognizes as proof.

Thinking about it more, I realized other people need more or less proof than I do about the exact same thing. My “unfortunate” namesake is a good example — Thomas, affectionately known as doubting Thomas. His bar for believing in the resurrection was higher than the other disciples': “Unless I see in His hands the mark of the nails, and place my finger into the mark, and my hand into His side, I will never believe.”

I just have to accept that. What's overwhelming proof to me might not be enough for someone else. I know the other side of it too — no one comes to Jesus unless the Father draws them, the god of this world has blinded them, the cross is foolishness to them, they suppress the truth in unrighteousness, and it's through the wisdom of the world that we fail to know God. So I pray. I pray their eyes would be opened. But I stay ready to give an answer for the hope that is in me.

It amazes me how much weight the word “believe” carries. Our very souls depend on what that word means. Stop and think for a second about the things you believe — the 49ers will make the Super Bowl this year, we never walked on the moon, Casey Anthony was guilty, nice guys finish last, the ball hit the ground on the Immaculate Reception. What goes through your mind with each one? Now think about betting on it — I'll bet $1, $100, $1,000, $1,000,000 that the 49ers make the Super Bowl. Something shifts, doesn't it? One more: do you believe every chair you sit in will hold you up? Would you sit in one with thin legs? One balanced on an inner tube floating down a river? One straddling the edge of a cliff? What went through your mind there?

I could graph what goes through my mind on this, especially about God, especially about the resurrection. A simple bar chart would show I need a score of 8 or above to believe there's a God. When I total up the evidence, I land on 1,836,433. Same story with the resurrection. Same story with the deity of Christ. Same story, honestly, with Puka Dog in Kauai being the best thing I've ever eaten.

Maybe that's where we should stay focused when we share Christ with a world that desperately needs Him — give people a good account for the hope inside us, then ask if they believe. If they don't, ask why not. Then ask: what would you need to believe this? What's stopping you?

I've never understood why Christians make such a big deal out of faith, like it's some spooky, unnatural thing. I get why unbelievers see it that way — they're picturing “blind” faith, the idea that there's no real reason to believe in God, or Christ, or salvation, but you put your faith in it anyway because of the good in your heart. That's crazy talk to me. I use the exact same everyday faith to sit in a chair that I use to trust Christ. Our faith in Him is loaded with solid proof, solid evidence — I'd even say more evidence than I have for sitting in a chair.

There are three words in the Bible that mean the world to me here, from Acts 1:3 — many infallible proofs. There are plenty of other ways Scripture says the same thing — what we've seen, heard, handled, the prophecies fulfilled, the testimonies — but those three words sum it all up for me. I don't need some mysterious faith to believe in God or trust in Christ when I already have many infallible proofs.

So let's rejoice today, and honestly every day, that Christ is risen. Our salvation is secure. We're His bride forever. The glory of the new heaven and new earth is ours forever. Reigning and serving Him will be our forever. He's made us kings and priests, forever.

What a glorious day this is! I can't wrap my head around it. But like my dear friend and brother Bill C says — I enjoy trying.

End of rant. I love you all. Thanks for always letting me rant, even when I repeat myself or sound arrogant.

— Erv
