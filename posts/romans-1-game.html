Romans 1. Let’s Play a Game.

Let’s play a game.

Pretend you are the Apostle Paul. You are near the end of a long, hard, but remarkably fruitful phase of ministry. You are at the end of your third missionary journey. You are likely in Corinth, around ten years before you will be executed by Nero. The Gentile churches have just taken a large offering for the poor believers in Jerusalem. You want to go there as soon as possible to deliver it — but then you also want to go to Rome and give them a gift, not a monetary one. You want to strengthen their faith through your teaching, the gospel ministry, and mutual encouragement. Jerusalem is roughly 800 miles east of Corinth. Rome is roughly 600 miles west. Spain is roughly 500 miles beyond Rome, and you hope to go there afterwards.

You have been praying for a long time that God would open a door for you to go to Jerusalem, Rome, and Spain. God has opened the door to Jerusalem. God has prevented you from going to Rome until now, because there were still unreached Gentile territories you had not gotten to yet (Romans 15:19–21).

You are not naïve about the danger waiting on the Jerusalem trip. In fact, you will ask the Roman Christians to pray for your safety and deliverance before you go. Which is exactly what will happen — the deliverance, that is, by the strangest route imaginable. You will be arrested in Jerusalem. You will get to Rome eventually; God will open the door. But you will go through it in custody, by way of a hazardous voyage in which your ship will be destroyed at sea.

After your arrest in Jerusalem you are taken to Caesarea, where you will sit in custody for at least two years. When you finally sail, the difficulties begin before you even reach Crete — weeks of fighting the wind. Then things get worse: a storm drives your ship for two weeks until it runs aground and breaks apart off an island called Malta, and you swim for your life to the shore. You will spend three months wintering on that island — where, by the way, your welcome includes a venomous snake fastening onto your hand. After that, one more ship, and you arrive in Rome about a month later. All told, it has been roughly three years since your arrest in Jerusalem. (The whole ordeal is in Acts 27–28.)

Let’s pause here. How are you feeling? You are finally in Rome! God has answered your prayer! On the other hand — it’s been three years! A terrible three years! Under arrest, in custody, in perilous conditions at sea, having swum to shore for your life, and greeted on the beach by a snakebite. Are you happier to see the believers in Rome, or fearful of your upcoming trial before Nero?

You are still under arrest, and will be for at least two more years. But at least it is house arrest — a rented lodging (at your own expense), not a public dungeon. You are still legally a prisoner, always under guard, and you cannot travel freely. But you can receive visitors and host meetings. You can preach, teach, counsel, and dictate letters, to a degree.

Now let’s stop and revisit what you prayed, back before any of this, in Romans 1:10–15:

“One of the things I always pray for is the opportunity, God willing, to come at last to see you. For I long to visit you so I can bring you some spiritual gift that will help you grow strong in the Lord. When we get together, I want to encourage you in your faith, but I also want to be encouraged by your faith. I want you to know, dear brothers and sisters, that I planned many times to visit you, but I was prevented until now. I want to work among you and see spiritual fruit, just as I have seen among other Gentiles. For I have a great sense of obligation to people in both the civilized world and the rest of the world, to the educated and uneducated alike. So I am eager to come to you in Rome, too, to preach the Good News.” (Romans 1:10–15, NLT)

Be honest. Are you thanking God for answering your prayer? Are you thanking Him for the many amazing ways He had to act on your behalf to get you here — to answer your prayer? Did you imagine it would be so difficult? So painful? Did you imagine that His answer would take so long? Are you thanking Him for His protection, and that neither the shipwreck nor the venomous snake killed you? Or are you upset? Shaking your fist at Him? Questioning Him? God, what was that? Seriously? That’s what You call an answer to my prayer — to come and bless Your children? To do what You asked me to do? Do You even care about how hard this was on me? Do I matter to You at all? Or were You even involved in any of this? Did You leave me hung out to dry? It seems to me that I saw more of Satan’s activity in all of this than Yours. What’s next? A brain tumor?

Now it is your turn, and I am not asking rhetorically. Put yourself on that rented floor in Rome, chained to a guard, prayer technically answered — and tell me honestly which of those two prayers is closer to the one in your chest. Email me if you like, but better: put it in the Comments below, so we can wrestle with it together. I will share where I have landed after I hear from you.
