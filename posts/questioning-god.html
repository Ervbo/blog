Questioning God

How are you doing? I'm doing a little better than normal — maybe only one or two percent better, but hey, I'll take that any day. I do have a little bit of PTSD — Pre-Traumatic Stress Disorder. I'm going to sell my 1978 CJ7. Long story, but maybe I can share it when our group resumes in a month. Can you believe it? We're only about a month away from starting Exile & Return. I can't wait. After my brain tumor I can really relate to the title of our study — or at least part of it. If BSF ever studies the books about my life, it would just be called Exile. So me and the Southern Kingdom have a lot in common. Actually, me and the Northern Kingdom have even more in common, because they never returned.

I've been pretty faithful studying Ezra, Nehemiah, Esther, Ezekiel, Daniel, Haggai, Zechariah, and Malachi ahead of time. Again, I have to say the podcasts AI put together for me have been amazing — I got so much more out of my study by listening to all of them.

I just finished Malachi a couple days ago. Man, what a hard-hitting book. So convicting, at least for me. There have been a few periods in my life where I've shaken my fist at God and said things like:

  • Why don't You love me?
  • Why don't You love me anymore?
  • I hate the church — the local one.
  • I'm a better father than You are, God.
  • Why did You let me suffer so much for doing what You asked me to do?

You'll be happy to know I haven't said any of these things again. In fact, I still feel the shame right now just typing them — it's a disgrace, really. But here's the thing — many of these are almost exactly what Israel said to God in Malachi. And it's fascinating to see how God answers them. Why didn't I read Malachi back when I was in the middle of doubting and accusing Him? I finally understand why I was voted “Most likely to get struck by lightning” in high school.

God bless you all. — Erv
