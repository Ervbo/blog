Hell. First Reaction.

What is a more triggering word than hell? What is a more controversial topic? Nevertheless, as Christians we need to understand exactly what the Bible says about hell, and we dare not shrink back from telling our friends and loved ones about it. Jesus certainly did not shy away from it. I have written a couple of other posts about hell that can be found here and here. What I want to talk about here is a little deeper and more nuanced. It is about suffering in hell.

No matter the reasons why God would send anyone to hell, one thing is certain. Hell is not a good place. It is not a happy place. It is a place of suffering. Unimaginable suffering. I know what you are thinking. That is for sure! Fire. Sulfur. Darkness. Silent. Tormenting. Fearful. Unquenchable. Demonic. Bottomless. But here is what I want to share with you. Those are the symptoms of what is the core of this suffering. What is inside all these symptoms.

I was reading through Genesis many years ago. What an amazing book of the Bible! Especially the first 12 chapters. So much is happening that sets the trajectory of all of life that we know today. Which is interesting, because what I am about to share with you seems more like a footnote or an afterthought to the main narrative. Or it did to me.

It’s about Cain and Abel. Adam and Eve’s first two children. You know the story. The whole world knows this story. Abel was a shepherd and Cain was a farmer. One day both brought an offering, a gift, to the LORD. Cain brought some of his crops and Abel the best portions of his firstborn lambs. The LORD accepted Abel and his gift, but He did not accept Cain and his gift. This made Cain very angry. You know what happens next. Because of his anger he ambushes his brother and kills him. There is a lot to unpack here. Why did God not accept Cain and his gift? Why did Cain kill Abel instead of God? That’s not what I want to focus on here. Look at what happens immediately afterwards:

“But the Lord said, ‘What have you done? Listen! Your brother’s blood cries out to me from the ground! Now you are cursed and banished from the ground, which has swallowed your brother’s blood. No longer will the ground yield good crops for you, no matter how hard you work! From now on you will be a homeless wanderer on the earth.’” (Genesis 4:10–12, NLT)

God immediately confronts Cain for what he has done. He curses him and banishes him from the land, his home. The curse is severe. Cain’s livelihood as a farmer was the ground, the soil. It would “no longer yield good crops for you, no matter how hard you work!” Think about that. Simply put it means: no matter what you do, no matter how hard you work for the rest of your life, you will survive — but only at the poverty level. How would you feel if that same curse was put on you? One would think the story stops there. But notice Cain’s response:

“Cain replied to the Lord, ‘My punishment is too great for me to bear! You have banished me from the land and from your presence; you have made me a homeless wanderer. Anyone who finds me will kill me!’” (Genesis 4:13–14, NLT)

He states: my punishment is too great to bear! You have banished me from the land. You have banished me from Your presence. You have made me a homeless wanderer. Anyone who finds me will kill me! All those conditions are terrible. It’s easy to see that. But was it really ‘too great to bear’? Here are my thoughts.

Each curse by itself has guaranteed a fruitless life of pain and suffering for Cain. The sum of all four makes the suffering exponential. But I believe there is only one of the four that Cain is reacting so strongly to. Only one of the four that makes his punishment too great to bear. And he tells us which one, specifically: “You have banished me from Your presence.” This is the one that no human can endure.

This brings me back to our subject. What does suffering in hell look like? My answer is simply: you have been banished from God’s presence. That is enough suffering in and of itself. It is the root of all the other suffering one would experience. Just knowing this is enough for anyone to realize that all of the other conditions — fire, darkness, weeping, and torment — are only secondary effects. The one thing that will hurt most in hell is knowing that you have been banished from God’s presence.

And lest you think I am building all of this on one verse in Genesis, the New Testament says it in so many words. When Paul describes the final judgment, here is the sentence he writes: “They will be punished with eternal destruction, forever separated from the Lord and from his glorious power” (2 Thessalonians 1:9). Forever separated from the Lord. Cain felt one lifetime of it and cried out that it was too great to bear. That is what hell is.
