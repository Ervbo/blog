Are You a Christian?

This is the most important question you can ever answer. Because the answer determines the destiny of your eternal soul (see my previous posts on the Soul).

Unlike other religions that leave your salvation as TBD, Christianity teaches that you can know if you are saved and going to heaven right now! So, let’s do that.

The first thing you must do is answer the question. There are only 3 choices: Yes / Not sure / No. For now, let’s focus on ‘Yes.’ The next logical question is, “how do you know?” Here there are numerous choices but only one correct one. There are verses too numerous to count, and I will put many of them at the end of this post, but let’s go with probably the most famous, well-known verse — John 3:16:

“For this is how God loved the world: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but have eternal life.” (John 3:16, NLT)

This is the answer. Is this the answer you gave? The reason I ask is that people come to Christ in many ways. Sometimes the person sharing with them will tell them to just say a prayer and oftentimes will lead them in it, helping them in what to say. Nothing wrong with this but it could leave someone believing it was that prayer alone that made them a Christian. Meaning they did not understand that it is linked to a new life. We will talk more about that in a coming post.

Sometimes, for other reasons, people come to Christ on their own believing they know the answer but are mistaken. Many will say they are a Christian because they are good, or better than most at least. They go to church. When they were younger, they served as altar boys/girls. They were baptized when they were babies. They always use their turn signal in the car. Again, nothing is wrong with these things but that still is not the answer.

I could list more of these nuances, but you can see where I am going. There is only one answer. I am a Christian because I believe in Christ. That is it! We are done. Well… not quite. There is nuance even in that. Like my examples above, someone might have told you that is the only answer but didn’t really explain what “believe in” Christ meant. That is the danger of taking one verse out of the Bible and hanging so much weight on it without knowing the context. The Bible is clear that believing Christ once existed, that He was a good man, and that He taught some great things, is not what is meant here. James is especially clear on this in James 2:19–20:

“You say you have faith, for you believe that there is one God. Good for you! Even the demons believe this, and they tremble in terror. How foolish! Can’t you see that faith without good deeds is useless?” (James 2:19–20, NLT)

He uses the most extreme example he can think of to make his point. Even the demons believe that God exists — and they tremble. He then talks about good deeds, which are linked to a new life — which we will get into in that coming post.

I have already written a few posts about what the Bible says about faith and belief — two words that are used interchangeably throughout the entire Bible: Faith Is a Load-Bearing Word, Believe vs. Belief, What More Proof Do You Need?, and Do Not Be Unbelieving, but Believing — and now a full slide deck: The Architecture of Biblical Faith.

But for now, let me say it in the way I understood it when I was saved. Do you believe in your parents? You say, “of course I do!” So, you are saying that they exist, or are good people? You say, “yes! Or not exactly! More than that.” What more? “I believe in them — in their character, that they will always be there for me, that they will do what they say, that they love me unconditionally, and that whatever they set out to do they will give it everything they have.” That is the faith and the belief God wants you to have in Him. That is saving faith. That is saving belief.

And that is why the Apostle John — the same John who wrote John 3:16 — could end his first letter this way:

“I have written this to you who believe in the name of the Son of God, so that you may know you have eternal life.” (1 John 5:13, NLT)

The important thing is for you to know you are a Christian. Know that you are saved. Know that you have eternal life. Here are more verses if you need them — read them slowly, and let the Bible interpret itself: John 1:12; John 3:36; John 5:24; John 6:47; John 10:27–29; Acts 16:31; Romans 8:1; Romans 8:38–39; Romans 10:9–13; 2 Corinthians 5:17; Ephesians 2:8–9; 1 John 5:11–13.
