Anguish in Prayer

Jon called our time together Tuesday night “precious,” and I've been turning that word over ever since.

The conversation went to some heavy places — first Scott's cancer diagnosis, then Bill's son Drew. It was overwhelming enough that I had to step out of the meeting for a minute, nearly in tears. I kept thinking of that Jack Handy line:

Chris's closing prayer that night was one of the more emotionally honest ones I've heard from him, and I'm grateful for it.

It got me thinking about the difference between bearable pain and unbearable pain. Bearable pain is eating a 12x12 at In-N-Out. Unbearable pain is watching your mother die of alcohol-related dehydration, alone in a trailer, left untreated by the man she was living with. That's mine.

I landed on 2 Corinthians 7:3 — “we carry you in our hearts” — though I'll admit the KJV and NKJV don't actually use the word “carry.” The Amplified comes closest, using “nested.”

That word sent me into a longer reflection on what prayer is actually doing:

  1. Prayer is relationship, not just petition — it's communication with a Person (Ps 116:1–2).
  2. Prayer is listening as much as speaking.
  3. Prayer is a kind of trick God uses on us — He moves us to act by first moving us to pray, so that we become the answer to our own prayer.
  4. Prayer forces us to internalize other people into our hearts. Think of Job's friends sitting with him in silence for seven days (Job 2:12–13) — before they said a single word, they were already praying with their presence.
  5. Prayer forces us to think about what Jesus would pray for, which slowly conforms us to His image (John 11:41; 1 John 5:14–15).
  6. The heavy heart itself is another trick — God's way of making us feel someone else's pain so we'll actually bear one another's burdens (Gal 6:2), and not just in the abstract. Sometimes that burden-bearing looks exactly as unglamorous as it sounds — I've literally helped a friend deal with a backed-up septic system because of it (2 Cor 1:3–4; Col 1:24).
