God and Breathing

Brothers, I am serious — take a deep breath right now. I am pretty sure none of you have heard what I am about to share with you before. I hadn't!

A friend sent me a 30-second video clip from a Hebrew theologian that turned my whole idea of “breath” upside down. I've been buried in Koine Greek so I can read the New Testament in its original language, and this clip made me think I need to pick up Hebrew too.

Before you watch it, sit with this for a minute:

  • Genesis says the Lord God formed man from the dust of the ground, then breathed the breath of life into his nostrils, and the man became a living being.
  • The Hebrew word ruach means “breath,” “wind,” or “spirit” — it's used for both the Spirit of God and the breath that animates every living creature.
  • The Greek word for “spirit” in the New Testament, πνεῦμα (pneuma), literally means “breath,” “wind,” or “air in motion,” coming from the verb πνέω (pneō), “to breathe” or “to blow.”
  • At creation, God's Spirit — His breath — hovered over the waters, and by that breath everything came into existence.
  • Jesus breathed on His disciples and said, “Receive the Holy Spirit” (John 20:22), handing them new spiritual life and empowering their mission.
  • That breath never stopped. The Holy Spirit keeps filling and empowering believers, which means the presence of God is as close as our own next breath.
  • Job put it plainly: “The Spirit of God has made me; the breath of the Almighty gives me life” (Job 33:4).
  • Psalm 150:6 goes even further: “Let everything that has breath praise the Lord” — the very ability to live and worship is sustained by God's breath.
  • And Scripture itself is described the same way — “All Scripture is God-breathed” (2 Timothy 3:16), carrying His authority because it carries His breath.

On the secular side, it turns out breathing isn't just how we stay alive — practiced consciously, it's a genuine tool for managing stress, supporting heart and lung health, improving sleep, and building emotional resilience. The first thing you ever do in life is breathe. The last thing you'll do in life is breathe. And there's a good chance you'll do it pretty consistently in between.
