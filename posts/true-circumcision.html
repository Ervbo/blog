True Circumcision

People seek many ways to validate that they have a right relationship with God. Most of them, if not all of them, are external. Here is a list of some that come to mind: church attendance, baptism, christening, confirmation, Bible knowledge, altar boy/girl, and various acts of service. But there are subtle ones as well: prosperity, success in any form, fewer problems or difficulties, and the favor and support of people. (I made a similar list in Are You a Christian? — this post is that one’s older cousin.) It has always been this way. But there is one way that is oddly different. Circumcision. Circumcision was given by God to mark His chosen people. The Jews. A seal, a pledge, a ratification. That they would obey the Abrahamic covenant to serve God and live a blameless life. But just like the other ways mentioned, it became a source of pride, and the external was emphasized over its internal agreement.

You can imagine a gathering of people eating lunch together after church. The topic of leadership comes up, and people start to comment on certain individuals. One person starts with — we are fortunate to have such a godly pastor. Who knows the Bible so well. And can teach it so well. Another responds — yes, but don’t forget our choir director. She sings like an angel. Another responds — let’s not forget the volunteers that keep the church running. Sunday school teachers, the administrators, and even the maintenance and custodian individuals. The conversation turns in a slightly different direction and one person says — you know, I was christened as a baby. I was fortunate. Another added — yes, me too. I was also confirmed a few years after that. I was even an altar boy. Another responded — I have not had any problems in my life. My marriage is strong and my children never rebelled in any way, even in their teenage years. Another responded — that is like my life. I have had success at everything I have done. Especially in school and in my career. The discussion dies down a little, and the group can tell one person has been unusually quiet. His name is Ira, and he finally speaks up and says — I am circumcised. Ever since I was 8 days old. I even have pictures of before and after. My mom saved the foreskin in a jar that she keeps in the kitchen. It is there today.

We can all probably relate to that hypothetical situation. Except for the last individual. That is not a normal occurrence. At least in our society today. But it was many years ago. What we read in the Bible and know to be historically true is that the Jews were very proud to be Jews. They are to this day. They were proud to be God’s ‘chosen people.’ And the way to prove it was to announce their crowning mark. Which, although unusual, is like the other ways in that by itself — with no internal connection to the promise it signifies — it is just a fashion statement.

But the Bible says something fascinating about circumcision in the NT, in Romans 2. It states:

“For you are not a true Jew just because you were born of Jewish parents or because you have gone through the ceremony of circumcision. No, a true Jew is one whose heart is right with God. And true circumcision is not merely obeying the letter of the law; rather, it is a change of heart produced by the Spirit. And a person with a changed heart seeks praise from God, not from people.” (Romans 2:28–29, NLT)

Wow. This is powerful. Not only in what it addresses about the Jewish people, but about all the individuals I have used as examples in this post. A true Jew is not one outwardly but inwardly. It is not the external removal of the flesh which defines their relationship to God, but the internal removal of the ‘flesh’ that covers their heart. Performed by the Spirit and not by an act of man. And it is revealed in an individual whose praise is not from men, but from God. Which is exactly the opposite of merely external circumcision. And lest you think Paul invented this internal reading — Moses and Jeremiah said it first: “The LORD your God will change your heart” — literally, circumcise your heart (Deuteronomy 30:6) — and “change your hearts before the LORD” (Jeremiah 4:4). The sign was never supposed to travel alone.

That is who I want to be! Don’t you? I have been baptized. I always go to church. I know the Bible incredibly well. Does that confirm that I have a relationship with God? Or the quality of that relationship? Possibly. But I choose to have my heart circumcised daily (if needed) by the Holy Spirit. A heart that breaks at what breaks God’s heart. A heart that rejoices in what God’s heart does. A heart that seeks God’s praise and not man’s.
