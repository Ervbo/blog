Prepared. But for What?

I've been working on a second children's book, and writing it has forced me to actually answer a question I'd always accepted a stock answer to: why doesn't God take us to heaven the instant we choose Him?

The common answer, and I think it's a true one as far as it goes, is that God leaves us here to know Him, to tell others about Him, to be salt and light, and to go through testing that strengthens our faith and builds godly character. A good analogy is marriage — God made us to see whether we'll say "I do," but He also made us to live with Him daily, to know and love Him more, and to become one spirit with Him, not just to make a one-time decision and then vanish into eternity.

But something about that answer always felt incomplete to me, and writing this book made me push further. Genesis 2:15 is pretty clear that God put Adam in the garden to work it, before sin ever entered the picture. Work isn't a curse-era add-on; it's part of the original design. So if God doesn't take us to heaven immediately, one reason is that He wants us to work — but not everyone is called to the same work, and everyone seems to get a different set of lessons and gifts along the way. What's that variation actually for?

Here's where I've stretched my theology further than I'm fully certain is correct, but it's the reason the book exists. What if earth isn't just proving ground for whether we'll choose God, and it isn't just a relationship we're meant to deepen — what if it's also specifically preparing us for work we'll do in heaven itself? Scripture says we're a kingdom of priests, and that when the New Jerusalem comes to earth, we'll reign with Christ on earth. We'll even judge angels. That's not passive rest. That's work, in eternity, that presumably requires skills, character, and capacities built somewhere first.

Keith Green's old tract asked it well: if you don't love, enjoy, and spend time with God now, what makes you think you will in heaven? I want to push that one step further — what if what God builds, teaches, and develops in us here doesn't stay behind when we go? What if it goes with us into eternity, into whatever that reigning and judging actually looks like?

I'll be honest about where this gets hard for me personally. If that's true, then my brain tumor and the roughly fourteen-year trial that's followed it should be preparing me for something. And I genuinely don't see what that something is. I don't see anything I've learned from it, or anything God has developed in me through it, that has benefited me or anyone else so far. If anything, I can point to more damage than growth. Either there's something in heaven that needs exactly what I'm being given here — something I have no visibility into yet — or my analogy simply breaks down at this step, and I need to keep thinking.

I don't have a clean ending for this one. It's a working theology, not a settled one. But it's the reason I'm writing the book, and it's the honest question underneath a lot of my prayers lately: prepared, yes — but for what, exactly?
