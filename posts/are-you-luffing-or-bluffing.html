Are you Luffing or Bluffing?

Here's that little essay I wrote a long time ago about what I felt was God helping me understand what “be filled with the Spirit” actually means. Remember, the Greek word for Spirit is pneuma — air. — Erv

Do you ever feel like you're stagnant in your Christian life? That you're not bearing fruit? That you no longer feel close to God, or aren't experiencing peace and joy, or maybe even that God has left you? I've felt all of these in my 40-plus years walking with the Lord. During those times I'd ask myself, then ask the Lord, why do I feel this way? Usually I'd quickly realize something was off with me — along the lines of, if you don't feel close to God, guess who moved. So I'd pray, “Father, I want to draw near to You. Help me see and put aside every sin in my life. Help me humbly receive Your Word and abide by it” (James 1:21, 22, 25; 4:18). That would get me back on track, but it wasn't until recently that I could see and fully grasp what was actually going on. That's what I'd like to share.

What God gave me was a new understanding of one of the most familiar verses in the Bible: “And do not get drunk with wine, for that is dissipation, but be filled with the Spirit” — Ephesians 5:18. I know you were probably hoping for something more profound, but hang with me here.

If you're like me, you've known this verse for a long time. You know it's fundamental to living the Christian life, and deep down you know it's what you need and ultimately desire. But also like me, you've probably been a little confused about what it really means, and even more so, how to actually do it. It's a bit like being told, “be filled with happiness today,” or “be filled with hope,” or “be filled with lunch.” You'd say, “yes, thank you very much” — and then think, what do they actually mean? That it'll just come my way? That I should do certain things to make it come my way? Or that they didn't mean anything practical at all and it was just a nice sentiment? So what did Paul really mean?

I'm not a theologian, so let me borrow some wisdom from Andrew Wilson here. In the biblical languages there's just one word for spirit, breath, and wind — ruach in Hebrew, pneuma in Paul's Greek. In English we split that into three separate words: breath, spirit, and wind. So we don't naturally connect being filled with the Spirit to being filled with breath or wind. In fact, many of us probably picture “filled with the Spirit” in terms of liquid, like a glass being filled with water — which raises odd questions. Why aren't we full already? Do we leak? Can the Spirit be spilled?

But Paul's original wording — filled with the Spirit, breath, wind — would have called to mind being filled with air. Think of a pipe or a trumpet, which only fulfills its purpose when filled with breath. Or better yet, a sailboat, which needs a power beyond itself — the wind — to go anywhere at all. Paul is using a dynamic image, not a static one. A glass of water only needs filling once; a sailboat is useless without continual filling.

If we start there and apply it to anything driven by air, we start to get at what Paul meant. Let's take sailing. Sailing is both a science and an art of keeping your sails full of wind — there's always a best orientation of boat and sail to move forward. Two terms come up constantly in that pursuit: luffing and telltales. In sailing, luffing refers to when a vessel is steered too far toward the wind, or the sail is eased too far past its optimal trim, so that airflow over its surfaces is disrupted and the sail begins to flap, or “luff.” This isn't always an error — sails will luff naturally when the bow crosses through the wind's direction as the boat tacks. Telltales show what's happening with the wind as it moves over the luff of the sail. Instead of waiting for the sail to luff and slow you down, telltales let you correct the trim before it ever flaps.

Do you see where I'm going? Another way of understanding what Paul is saying is this: always be like a good sailor, orienting your boat and sails to get the most out of the wind, always alert to catch and correct any luffing before it happens. Orient your life, your heart, your emotions and will so you're always being driven fully by the Holy Spirit. Watch your telltales, and pray for God to keep you from luffing in your moment-by-moment voyage with Him.
