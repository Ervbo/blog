God Draws Near. Part 2.

Part 2 of the God Draws Near series.

This is Part 2 of God Draws Near. Part 1 is here.

After God has delivered Israel from their bondage in Egypt, we see Him take another step in being closer to His people. He gives them the Ten Commandments. Then His laws of justice and mercy. And He makes a covenant with them to bring them into a land He has prepared for them — the Promised Land. Finally, He commands them to build the Tabernacle:

“Then have them make a sanctuary for me, and I will dwell among them. Make this tabernacle and all its furnishings exactly like the pattern I will show you.” (Exodus 25:8–9)

And after detailing the offerings and sacrifices He requires, and consecrating the priests to represent the people, He ends with this:

“So I will consecrate the tent of meeting and the altar and will consecrate Aaron and his sons to serve me as priests. Then I will dwell among the Israelites and be their God. They will know that I am the LORD their God, who brought them out of Egypt so that I might dwell among them. I am the LORD their God.” (Exodus 29:44–46)

God is now dwelling amid Israel! This is a big step from where He was after Adam and Eve sinned and were expelled from His presence in the Garden. But it doesn’t end there. The Tabernacle is made to be mobile. It will go with the people as He leads them to the Promised Land. It is there that the Temple will be built, and God will truly be settled among His people.

However, before we get to that, I want to talk about something I skipped over. You will undoubtedly remember that after God had given Moses the Ten Commandments — written on stone tablets with His very own finger — the Israelites had made and were worshiping a golden calf as their deliverer! This made Moses so angry that he threw down the tablets, breaking them into pieces. God was also angry, and threatened to destroy all of Israel! Can you imagine? He loves Israel! They are His chosen people! Now He wants to destroy them. Fortunately, Moses intercedes for them and God relents. But notice what He says afterwards:

“Go up to the land flowing with milk and honey. But I will not go with you, because you are a stiff-necked people and I might destroy you on the way.” (Exodus 33:3)

Here is God, who has put into place a plan of redemption for His people, just moments after they have chosen that they preferred their own will to His — essentially saying they did not want Him and to leave them alone. He has made Himself known to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. He has delivered them from a long, cruel bondage to the Egyptians with incredible signs and wonders. Now He is saying: enough! I will destroy you. I will not go with you.

It needs to be said here why God seems to have had this sudden change of heart. And pay attention to this, because it reveals an even deeper love for His people than we realize — and probably don’t even realize in your own life at times. I know what you are thinking. You are right! I never thought how greatly He must love His people, and me myself, that He would want to destroy them — or even me. I know it is easy to think that, because it is not obvious what is going on underneath this. At least it wasn’t for me the first few times I read it. Now listen carefully to what I am about to say, because what we see here has impacted my walk with God, my relationship with Him, almost more than anything else. God loves His people. God loves you. That can never be in question. But when I said up front that “now He wants to destroy them,” I deliberately used an inaccurate word. The word wants. The more accurate word should be must. Now you are thinking — yes, Erv… that is much more comforting.

Let me explain. God is perfect. Perfect in every way — in all His attributes, and in how His attributes are balanced. He is holy. God is not a man. He changes not. He is immutable. A man is not perfect in any way — in all his attributes or how they are balanced. Even if he were, he changes. At the drop of a hat. He is mutable. He may be the most loving, kind person for years, and then, due to whatever circumstances, begins kicking puppies and pulling cats’ tails. Never think of God like that! He is 100% constrained by His attributes. He must always do who He is! Stop and let that sink into your heart as far as it can go. He could never fail you, hurt you, leave you… even if He wanted to! I know that sounds absurd, but I chose to say it that way to drive this point home as far as I can.

And this is exactly what we see here with Him and His people, the Israelites. Let me try to rephrase the situation. God has seen His people. While up on Mount Sinai instructing Moses how to build the Tabernacle so that He could dwell with His people, while writing the Ten Commandments on stone tablets to give them… God is seeing His people. When you are Almighty God, you can do things like that. God is the ultimate multitasker! He is seeing the Israelites worshiping this golden calf. But here is where the beautiful but not obvious nuance comes in. He does say:

“I have seen these people, and they are a stiff-necked people. Now leave me alone so that my anger may burn against them and that I may destroy them. Then I will make you into a great nation.” (Exodus 32:9–10)

But this is what He is really saying: I love My people. When I love, there is no greater love. A nursing mother may forget, even abandon her child, but I could never forget nor abandon My people. But I am holy. Holiness requires an abhorrence and removal of anything unholy, sinful. That is why I am instructing Moses in how to build My Tabernacle and what is required of the priests. I can’t dwell among you if that is not addressed. My holiness will simply not allow it. You can’t fully appreciate how strongly My love draws Me to you. And you can’t fully appreciate, at the same time, how strongly My holiness repels you from Me. The reason I can no longer go with you is not because I am angry, or because I do not want to. The reason is that I know how sinful you are, and I would have to destroy you, because of the holiness of My nature.

Let’s leave it there for now. Because, as we shall see, God’s plan of redemption has not changed — and His desire to dwell with His people in the Promised Land has not changed either.
