Letters to the Brothers

During our BSF year in the book of Revelation (2024–25), I wrote to the men in my study group most weeks — letters that always began the same way: Brothers. They were never meant to be blog posts. They were written to a couple dozen men I love, about whatever God had put in my head that week: lesson questions, word studies, books, videos, jokes, and the occasional thunderbolt. But rereading them, I realized they belong in this Notebook — they are the seedbed a lot of my later writing grew from. So here they are, published as they were written and dated as they were sent, lightly edited only to protect the group’s privacy and to fix links for public reading.

The letters

  1. The Bible. Mathematics. Logic. (Feb 11, 2025)
    The only 4 things I know for certain in life — and a free Hillsdale course covering the first three.
  2. Eschatology via Revelation (Feb 18, 2025)
    A chronological timeline of the end-times events, with Revelation’s chapters placed within it.
  3. Jesus. Believe IN. (Mar 19, 2025)
    What it really means to ‘believe in’ someone — and why the English may hold up better than I thought.
  4. The Schemes of the Devil (Mar 29, 2025)
    Strategies for avoiding Satan’s deception — and why I always do my own answers first.
  5. The Books of God (Mar 29, 2025)
    Every book God keeps, from the tablets of stone to the Lamb’s Book of Life — plus the AI-built deck that would have made Andy Grove proud.
  6. God Draws Near. The Letter. (Apr 1, 2025)
    The seed that grew into the God Draws Near trilogy, a year before I knew it.
  7. 25 Iconic Bible Stories (Apr 4, 2025)
    On being ‘in love’ with the Word, and a checklist for Fellowship Night.
  8. The Bible and AI (Apr 10, 2025)
    After 49.5 years of searching for the perfect way to study the Bible: constrain the AI to Scripture alone.
  9. One Nation Under God (Apr 15, 2025)
    Philippians 1:15–18 at Easter — the hardest passage I ever memorized.
  10. Holy Spirit (Apr 16, 2025)
    Spy Wednesday: a forgiven Judas, Philip Yancey, and Spurgeon’s thunderbolt on the Holy Spirit.

There are more letters in the archive — this page will grow as I publish them. To the Brothers: thank you for being the first readers of everything here. This Notebook exists because you kept reading. — Erv
