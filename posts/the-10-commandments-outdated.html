The 10 Commandments. Outdated?

I finished an App project this morning after working on it most days from 4am to noon for a couple of weeks. I really didn't want to let God, my pastor, or myself down on this one. Once that was off my plate, I had a question rattling around that I almost posted straight to Facebook: which of the Ten Commandments do people still believe are actually wrong to break? Before posting, I decided to check what the data actually says.

The pattern that came back was striking, and honestly not surprising once I saw it laid out. Most Americans, regardless of religious belief, still treat about half the list as non-negotiable. Strong majorities say murder, stealing, lying (bearing false witness), adultery, and dishonoring parents remain clearly wrong — adultery in particular consistently ranks near the top of "most immoral behaviors" in national surveys. Coveting still gets majority agreement too, though a bit softer.

But the commandments that are explicitly about God — not other people — are treated very differently. "You shall have no other gods before me," "you shall not make an idol," "you shall not take the Lord's name in vain," and especially "remember the Sabbath and keep it holy" get far less traction. The Sabbath command polls lowest of all — only around half of Americans consider it still important, and plenty treat it as fully optional.

What that split tells me is something I hadn't quite put into words before: the horizontal commandments — the ones about how we treat each other — have mostly survived secularization intact, because they map onto something like a shared human conscience even outside any belief in God. But the vertical commandments — the ones that are only "wrong" if God actually exists and actually deserves that place in your life — get quietly dropped the moment culture stops assuming He's there. That's not really a surprise theologically. It's exactly what you'd expect if the first four commandments were the hinge the other six swing on, rather than six independent moral rules bolted onto four religious add-ons.

So are the Ten Commandments outdated? Culture's answer, in practice, seems to be: half of them, no; half of them, basically yes. Which tells me less about whether the commandments are outdated and more about how much of our morality is still borrowed from a worldview a lot of people have quietly stopped believing in.
