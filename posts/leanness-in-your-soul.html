Leanness in your soul

I want to share an unusual verse I don't think I've ever brought up before — not from a pastor, a Bible teacher, or even our own church fathers. I never hear it referenced anywhere. I love the Bible enough that I've never stopped reading and memorizing it since the moment I was saved, and when I retired at fifty I only ratcheted that up further. As a result I regularly stumble across odd corners of Scripture nobody talks about.

I was recently scrolling Christian content on Instagram and came across a post asking which of the twelve disciples Jesus put in charge of the finances. Scrolling through the answer, I realized it touches on exactly the verse I mean:

They soon forget his works; they waited not for his counsel: but lusted exceedingly in the wilderness, and tempted God in the desert. And he gave them their request; but sent leanness into their soul.

— Psalm 106:13–15

Two things jumped out at me the first time I read it. First: you'd expect the story to read like a victory lap. Israel had just been miraculously delivered from Egypt, walked through the Red Sea, and was on its way to the Promised Land behind the Ark of the Covenant. The God of the universe had answered their prayers in the most dramatic way imaginable. You'd expect “Hallelujah, great is our God, He divided the sea to save us” — and instead we get the opposite. They forgot His works. They didn't wait for His counsel. Instead: we can figure this out ourselves, we just need some meat, this manna is basically rice cakes, one more sliver and we'll be sick of it, give us pheasant under glass. They lusted exceedingly and tempted God to prove He still cared.

The second thing floored me even more: He gave them their request. A patient, understanding God, granting rebellious people exactly what their flesh craved instead of what He'd already provided. And that's where “leanness of soul” comes in. Their stomachs got exactly what they demanded — and their souls started spiritually starving and wasting away, precisely because that's what they'd asked for.

Be careful what you ask for.
