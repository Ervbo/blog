I AM. Yes.

I have been a faithful student of God’s Word since the day I became a Christian, when I was 17 years old. I have read it consistently, I have studied it consistently, and I have memorized large portions of it. I have also sat at the feet, if you will, of most of the church forefathers and patriarchs, reading both their writings and their biographies. I have led Bible studies weekly most of my adult life. Finally, I have sat at the feet of Dallas Theological Seminary and their numerous professors and authors. Legendary. Why am I bringing this up? Certainly not to prop up my ego or lord it over anyone. It is simply to introduce my topic for today. You see, I think about God’s Word constantly, outside of my times reading it. I sometimes wonder if that is not the greatest benefit I receive from God’s Word — its ‘secondary’ effect. Obviously, this is connected to Jesus’ promise that when the Spirit comes, “he will teach you everything and will remind you of everything I have told you” (John 14:26). Here is how that played out last night.

Lately I have been thinking about God’s name: I AM. YHWH. (I need to write an entire post about that name in the future — it deserves one. I circled it once before, in Who Do You Say He Is?, and deliberately saved it.) I have also been thinking a lot about my free will. My ability to make a choice — about anything. Specifically about God. My choice to choose Him. To attach or connect to Him. To love Him. Here is how those thoughts came together, and how they unfolded.

Let’s start at the beginning. I mean the real beginning. What is the beginning of infinity? I don’t know, but that is where we must start. Genesis 1 and John 1 help us here, because they both start with “In the beginning…” Basically, Genesis states, “In the beginning… God,” and John 1 states, “In the beginning… the Word… God… Jesus.” To me, another way of saying all of that is: “In the beginning… I AM. YHWH.” Which makes sense. The reality is there is nothing that exists, or ever will exist, that does not originate with God and is not sustained by God. The infinite, eternal God. The Alpha and the Omega (Revelation 22:13). So let that be settled. Moving on, there is me. What do I know about me?

One day I showed up on planet earth. To my knowledge that is when I began to live, to be a human, and to have an eternal soul. At a young age, roughly 6 or 7 years old, I became acutely aware of myself. I don’t know how that looks in other people, or if it happens like it did to me. They say self-awareness is what separates humanity from animals. To me that is the same thing as saying having a soul is what separates us from animals (I wrote a whole series on the soul). My first awareness of my soul — an existential epiphany — happened while walking into my house. Nothing unusual about that, but it was the thought that hit me: “I am here.” And it hit hard. The best way I can describe it is like the feeling you get on a giant rollercoaster when it makes that first huge drop and it feels like your heart is in your throat. It was like all my insides were shaking whenever that thought replayed itself. So let that be settled. Not that I was an eternal soul — but that for the first time I knew it. (Looking back now, I smile at what my soul’s first words were. A little ‘i am’ waking up inside the great I AM.) But what about that is important?

At that moment there was nothing particularly important about it. That would come much later — more specifically, when I became a Christian at 17 years old. Simply stated, that is when I met God. I asked Him into my life, into my heart. He was now on the throne. He was first and foremost in my life. I was eternally and irreversibly connected to Him. As I began living my new life, I became aware that everything in and of my flesh is destroyed forever. Only what I do for and through Christ lasts. In this life all my decisions would have a great impact on me. Where I went to college, what I majored in, who I married, where I decided to live, what career I would go into, how many children I would have, what ‘good’ things I chose to do, what ‘bad’ things I chose to do… But the impact, the effects, would only be in my lifetime, and then be gone forever. So where does that leave me?

I am surprised at the utter simplicity of the answer to that question when it came to me. All of everything, eternally, can be boiled down to 2 things. 1) I AM. God always was, always is, and always will be. That will never change. Everything will exist and continue because of this. 2) Me. I am alive now, and I can only do one thing to affect my eternity — to narrow it down further, where I will spend my eternity. With God. Apart from God. Connected to God. Severed from God. That’s it. A choice. A Yes or a No. Why is that significant? Let me try and pull this all together.

God is. That will never change, in any way, forever. It is a given. When people ask, “Well, who created God then?” they have the question backwards. They should be asking, “If there was ever nothing, who created that?” You see the difference? As humans we are used to nothing being the default, the initial condition. There is nothing until something, or someone, comes along and puts it there. And builds on it in some way. So logically speaking, from our perspective there should be nothing. It’s as simple as that. From a completely intellectual, logical view, there should be nothing, anywhere, forever. This reminds me of the words of the famous mathematician and philosopher Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz: “The first question which we have a right to ask will be, ‘Why is there something rather than nothing?’” But the truth is the opposite. There is always something rather than nothing. There can never be ‘nothing’ unless there is something to create it. That something is God.

What about me? As I mentioned earlier, I can do a myriad of things in my life with this body God has given me, with the many choices that accompany it. But they will all be done with, and fade away, just like my body, after I die. But my body also has a soul. My body can make a choice about my soul. From everything in the Bible, we see our soul is eternal and precious to God (Psalm 116:15). If we choose, that soul can go to be with Him forever. To love and serve Him, to enjoy Him, and to worship Him. If that is what we truly want, He is more than happy to give that to us! If that is not what we want, He graciously gives us an alternative. It’s sad, but He is so loving He will not force us to be with Him or submit to Him — even though it’s what’s best for us and the right thing to do. (I wrote a whole post on that too: Heaven or Hell. Your Choice.)

What do you think of that? In the overwhelming complexity of life and existence in our infinite and eternal universe, everything boils down to only 2 things. Is there a God, and do you want to be with Him? The answers: I AM. Yes.
