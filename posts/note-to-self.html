Note to Self

Yesterday I had an apparent breakthrough in my ongoing quest to link Quantum Mechanics and Relativity — affectionately referred to as the Theory of Everything. The breakthrough was: do they even need to be linked at all? Or is this just something my mathematical brain ‘wants’? Maybe even ‘needs’?

Perhaps I posted my thoughts too quickly. I have always liked Dawson Trotman’s quote — “Thoughts disentangle themselves over the lips and through the fingertips.” So I am going to use this post to disentangle my thoughts through my fingertips.

First, I think I need to back up a little. As an engineer, you would expect me to prefer a world that is either black or white. And you would be right. I noticed this within myself at a very young age. I loved Math. I really did — I still do! For that reason: the clarity and the simplicity, adding to that the structured problem solving. However, as I matured and experienced more of life, it was clear that life was not black and white. Not even close. In my career at Aerojet and Intel it was. That was my safe place, where I could defeat ambiguity and live in peace.

I believe I was about ten years into my career at Aerojet when I had an epiphany — a paradigm shift in my thinking. I remember the moment, but I don’t remember what led to it. It was probably a long series of little things that finally reached a breaking point. The first shift was accepting Paradox in life. Not only that, but embracing it. When I did, I instantly felt a rush of wisdom come into my head like a cool breeze. I really did. My black-and-white perspective had shut out so many contradictory thoughts, but especially Paradoxes. Paradoxes tortured me! Hey, I feel a joke coming on. How do you torture an engineer? Give him a Paradox to solve.

There was another step in my thinking that happened shortly after that. It came from a great friend of mine. Very intelligent — his PhD is in Structural Mechanics. Very wise. We were at lunch and he told me this: “Life is more of an Art than a Science.” Immediately the restaurant started spinning and everyone started taking turns doing CPR on me! J/K! But seriously, it was hard for me to hear what he said, harder to receive it, and harder still to understand it. So I just replied — “That’s stupid.” J/K!

When you add those two major shifts in my thinking, it explains why I might have jumped the gun yesterday when I saw their possible application to the Theory of Everything.

One other thing should be said here. I had been exposed to the Chinese concepts of Tao and yin-yang in my early 20s, but mostly at a surface level. I knew Tao meant some sort of universal thought on something, and that yin-yang was just the observation that so many things in life exist paired with their opposites.

Here is why I am glad I am writing this post — I can feel my thoughts disentangling themselves now. I am going to dig a little into this Chinese philosophy…

The Tao is the whole, unified, ultimate reality — formless, nameless, the source of everything. But the moment that unity gives rise to the world we actually experience, it manifests as a dynamic interplay of two complementary forces: yin and yang (Wikipedia). Think of it this way:

  • Tao = the one, undivided source (“the Way”)
  • Yin-yang = the Tao in motion — the pattern by which the one becomes the many

The Tao Te Ching itself gets at this directly: “The Tao produced One; One produced Two; Two produced Three; Three produced All things.” That “Two” is yin and yang. Everything that exists is understood as some ratio or dance of these two forces — light/dark, active/passive, expanding/contracting, hot/cold — not fighting each other, but continuously generating and defining each other. Neither can exist without the other; there is no yang without yin to contrast it, and each contains a “seed” of the other, which is why the classic symbol has a small dot of white inside the black half and vice versa.

Wow! I wish I knew that before now. That explains a lot, and now I can see how all of this is coming together in my head — and why yesterday was such a huge breakthrough for me. Let me see if I can explain it in a simple bullet list.

  • At a very young age I saw how Math explained everything and made so much logical sense.
  • As I aged, I saw that Science did the very same thing.
  • The thought that I could discover the link between Quantum Mechanics and Relativity came to me.
  • In college it all came together, and I graduated with a Mechanical Engineering degree.
  • At the beginning of my aerospace career I discovered I was great at programming computers. Just like Math and Science, it was so logical and made so much sense. I need to say something here: it could have ruined me. Writing a computer program is essentially entering a perfect black-and-white world. I loved it. I still do! But it’s not real. It is entirely possible I would have ended up a social outcast, walled off in that perfect logical world, if I hadn’t added the insights mentioned above.
  • I accepted Paradox as a normal part of life — a normal part of the universe. I embraced it.
  • I accepted the fact that life is more of an Art than a Science. Which was so hard, given my strengths in the Math/Science area.
  • I had an epiphany: maybe Quantum Mechanics and Relativity don’t need to be linked. At least not in the way people — me included — think they should be, with a constant, a multiplier, or an additional term that goes away at large scales and emerges at small ones.
  • Building on that thought, I asked AI if anyone else had ever had similar thoughts. I listed nine things I had observed in the universe, in completely unrelated fields, that described something polarized like Quantum Mechanics and Relativity — needing no linkage, just a healthy tension in which both exist at the same time. To my surprise, the AI stated an emphatic “Yes!” and cited Niels Bohr — one of my favorite scientists. He had reached that exact same conclusion and wrote a paper on it. He even made himself a Coat of Arms describing it.

That conversation — my nine pairs and the full response, Bohr’s coat of arms included — is the previous post: It’s All About Duality. Note to self: the tension was never the enemy.
