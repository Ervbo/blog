The Giving Tree

What are your thoughts about unconditional love? Does it exist? Have you ever received it from someone? Have you ever given it to someone? When? For how long? Many people will say that the greatest thing in life is love — both to love and be loved. Do you agree with that? But what ‘type’ of love? It is unfortunate that there must be different definitions for love. Most of you reading this blog are probably Christian, so you are aware of the different Greek words for love:

Greek termBasic senseA practical example
Eros (ἔρως)Passionate, erotic, or strongly romantic desirePhysical attraction and intense romantic longing
Philia (φιλία)Affectionate friendship, loyalty, and goodwillThe bond between close friends or trusted companions
Storgē (στοργή)Natural familial affectionA parent’s love for a child or affection among family members
Agapē (ἀγάπη)Broad, generous, self-giving loveLove expressed through care for others, charity, or — in Christian usage — God’s self-giving love
Philautia (φιλαυτία)Self-loveHealthy self-respect — but also selfishness or vanity, depending on context
Xenia (ξενία)Hospitality and goodwill toward a guest or strangerThe ethical duty to welcome and protect visitors, especially important in Homeric culture

Beyond that, there is another way to define love:

FormulationWhat it meansUnderlying condition
“I love you if you…”Love is offered on the condition that the other person behaves, provides, agrees, changes, or performs in a required way.“You must meet my terms.”
“I love you when…”Love or warmth is expressed during favorable moments or moods.“I feel loving when circumstances are good.”
“I love you because…”Love is tied to qualities, benefits, achievements, attractiveness, kindness, or what the person contributes.“You have reasons that make you lovable to me.”
“I love you in spite of…”Love remains present despite acknowledged limitations, mistakes, disappointments, or hardship.“Your flaws do not cancel your worth or my care.”

I think it is safe to say that when people say “the greatest is love” they are referring to unconditional love. I think it is also safe to say that only God loves in that way. Although a mother’s love comes awfully close. The Bible is not only where we get the idea that “the greatest of these is love” (1 Corinthians 13:13), but also that God “is love” (1 John 4:8).

I know that Christianity did not invent the word agape. It was used before Christ was born. Which makes sense, because unconditional love is observed everywhere, at different times, and in different situations. I just mentioned tongue-in-cheek that a mother’s love is almost analogous to God’s love. But it should be noted that although Christianity did not invent the word, the New Testament gave it unusually central theological and ethical importance. And that also makes sense. Can you imagine what people must have thought about the word after Jesus lived it 24/7 for ~33 years? It must be like using the word ‘swim’ for people who could dog paddle over the centuries — and then one day a guy named Michael Phelps shows up. Is it even the right word to say Michael Phelps swims?

I am tempted to look at every instance in His life that exemplified — that screamed — ‘unconditional love’ to us. I am tempted because in my mind I can see the long list right now, and to dig deeply into each one would be a joy because of all the insights I know I would get. But I just want to highlight one that is almost always in my thoughts. Luke 23:34: “Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing.”

That will always turn my brain inside out. I have read the crucifixion of Jesus hundreds and hundreds of times. Every time, I think I discover something more to abhor. The list is endless. Seriously. You can spend your whole life just digging into the many ways, and the depth of each one, as to how Jesus suffered. And mocked! I added the mocking because that really is where I go crazy. You have heard the phrase ‘adding insult to injury.’ That is what this is, multiplied by a gazillion. How heartless and how evil can sin get?! Look at this man beaten to the very point of death, hanging on a cross — and you are going to mock Him? Oh man, I am freaking out even now as I write this. If I was there, you would have seen a crazy man responding in ways I am not even going to mention here.

Calming myself down a little — I am almost mad at what Jesus prays. FORGIVE THEM?! THEY DO NOT KNOW WHAT THEY ARE DOING?! Of course they do. It’s stated in all the Gospels. Look at Mark 3: the Pharisees immediately consult with the Herodians about how they might “destroy” Jesus (Mark 3:6). They did not just get up on the wrong side of the bed one day and decide to kill Him. Even when Pilate tried to let Him go, they cried all the louder, “Crucify Him! Crucify Him!”

Asking the Father to forgive them would not have even been a thought in my mind. My prayer would have been: “Father. Switcheroo. I ask for the blessed Switcheroo.” What that would have done is place me on the ground — and everyone that beat me, spat on me, plucked my beard out, shoved the crown of thorns on my head, flogged me just one whipping short of death, made me carry my cross, stripped me naked, drove the spikes into my feet and hands, lifted me up on that cross all while mocking me… they would be where I was. I would be where they were. That’s what I call the Blessed Switcheroo. And that is also why I am not God. Not that I was ever even in the running, but this excludes me once and for all.

Wow. I won’t be able to finish this today. I haven’t even gotten to the reason I went into so much detail about this ‘unconditional love’ and it being the defining attribute of God in Christ. Because all of this sets up something I desperately want to say about the famous children’s book The Giving Tree. I will calm down now and come back next week with that. I guarantee it will be much shorter and less emotional.
