The Third Commandment

But there is something in Hebrew that I want to discuss. It is very convicting though. You all know our good friend, Aaron — Moses' brother, the first high priest, the guy who wore the names of the 12 tribes of Israel on his ephod, over his heart (hey, I did that with my youth group for 30+ years). Remember what he had on his forehead? A gold plate. A gold plate that said, "HOLY TO THE LORD." How would you like to wear a plate like that on your forehead? When you walk around town. Go to the movies. At work. At 49ers games. During your Zoom meetings. Well, you do. You just didn't know it. But I get ahead of myself.

Do any of you know the Third Commandment offhand? Most of us remember it as, "You shall not take the name of the LORD your God in vain." We were taught that it meant — don't just say the word God randomly in casual conversation, and certainly don't add a profanity to it as a suffix. And that is partially correct. Partially. But not the emphasis.

While we are here, look at the consequence: "The LORD will not hold them guiltless." Heavy-duty stuff. We will get to that later.

In classical Jewish writings it was well known that you were never to swear by God's name falsely, for two reasons. Lying is a sin — plain and simple. But lying in God's name, you were also putting God's authority and His reputation as a personal guarantee that you were telling the truth. Essentially, you were dragging God's own credibility into your lie — putting Him as a co-signer on it.

Let's stop here and see that even with this, it is not just about saying "God" casually or adding profanity as a suffix.

The foundational meaning of the word "take" or "misuse" was — to lift, to carry, to bear a weight on your body. Remember this.

Bearing, carrying God's name falsely is the focus, and empty words are only one way to do it.

Exodus 19 says the whole nation of Israel is a kingdom of priests. They had the same assignment as Aaron! Every farmer at the edge of that camp, every mother at a well, every elder sitting at a gate, was wearing this same assignment in public.

The New Testament also calls Christians a kingdom of priests. Every accountant, contractor, machine operator, engineer, is wearing this same warning about what you carry into public view while claiming to represent Him.

And now the verdict — which should cause us all to stop and ask God to search our hearts, our lives. The verdict attached to this is loud and clear: the LORD will not hold him guiltless.

And think about the scope of this! How many wars are fought in God's name? How popular is the phrase, "Praise the Lord and pass the ammunition"? How about TV preachers that squeeze money from their flock in God's name — some older and helpless, barely surviving themselves?

I know we have talked many times about people we know and are close to us who we can see are not living the Christian life — or even trying. They wear the label "believer" publicly. They never cuss or use profanity. They show up every week at church. But they live in a way that quietly tells everyone watching that this God is not worth taking seriously. Their lives, not their words — their conduct gives the enemies of the LORD occasion to blaspheme (2 Samuel 12:14).

One final thing, and again, we've talked a lot about it: people who prophesied in His name, cast out demons in His name, performed wonders in His name, standing in front of Jesus Himself. What He said back to them is eerily similar to all of this: "No. I never knew you."
