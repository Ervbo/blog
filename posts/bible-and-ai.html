The Bible and AI

A letter to my BSF Brothers · April 10, 2025 · part of Letters to the Brothers.

I am excited to share with you this morning something I think will revolutionize your study of the Bible! Pretty much for 50 years (49.5 to be exact) I have been searching for the ‘perfect’ way to study God’s Word. I could easily write an entire thesis paper on what I have discovered. Maybe some other time. Even now I am hesitant to share it because I think it is just the tip of the iceberg! But you know me. I can’t stay quiet about the Bible! I was somewhat that way before my brain damage but afterwards it was apparent that it was worse. But let’s get into it.

(Skip the next paragraph if you don’t want my long introduction.)

From all that I know there is nothing like the Bible! It is by far the most precious thing that mankind possesses! I love that the most precious person man possesses is Jesus Christ. BOTH are called The Word! I was hardcore Bible from the very day after I became a Christian. I read the NT quickly, in a paraphrase version that my junior prom date gave me. I did not go to church — no one told me that is what Christians do. I had nobody to mentor or disciple me. The guy that led me to the Lord just went back into his own world. But after 3–4 months I felt like I knew the Bible well enough that I started a Bible study at lunch on my high school campus! 🙂 For the longest time it was just me and the Bible. It felt wrong to me to try to understand it with anything beyond my own mind, heart, and the Holy Spirit (1 John 2:27). Over time I changed my thinking — I began to look at other Christian writings as I did my Christian friends. They had tons of value in my life. “He who walks with wise men will be wise.” My Christian books were my friends. However, what did not change is the order of my study. I decided not to jump into a commentary before I studied the Bible, or run to one when I encountered a difficulty. I needed to not be lazy, do due diligence (hey, Erv said doo-doo), wrestle with it and seek the Holy Spirit’s guidance. I read all the references (way to go, NASB margin) and let the Bible interpret itself. When I found out there was an Exhaustive Concordance, I used that too. Before I turned to any other person, I wanted to study God’s Word in as pure a way as possible. I am so glad I did — because God warned me early on, “knowledge puffs up, but love builds up” (1 Cor 8:1).

With that foundation, here is how it all came together over the last few days:

  1. Constrain the AI to Scripture alone. Two of the AI tools I use most are Perplexity and NotebookLM. One of NotebookLM’s strengths is that you can constrain it to use only the resources you give it — docs, PDFs, web URLs, YouTube videos, your Grandma’s phone number… pretty much anything. I jumped on that, because I constrain myself to use only the Bible when I study it. I uploaded a PDF of the entire Bible. (One catch: the Bible is over 500,000 words, so I had to break it into OT and NT.)
  2. Ask it everything. I started asking every difficult question I could think of — tongues, the Rapture, how did Judas die, where do we see Jesus is God, grace and works, baptism, money… The beauty was apparent right away: since it was constrained to the Bible alone, I wasn’t being influenced or coerced by anyone’s tradition.
  3. A morning experiment. In my Quiet Time I uploaded just Matthew, Mark, Luke and John and asked: at approximately what time in Jesus’ life did He cleanse the temple? It gave the answer I expected — and then, boom, one I never expected: “based on the 4 Gospels you gave me, Jesus cleared the temple twice — once at the beginning of His ministry and once at the very end.” The only other option would be a contradiction in the Gospels — John vs. the Synoptics.
  4. And more. The Mind Map, Audio Overview (podcast), Briefing Document, Study Guide, Timeline and FAQ fill an entirely different niche in Bible study. Some other time.

I could go on but for now — can you all see the advantage of using AI in this manner? — Erv
