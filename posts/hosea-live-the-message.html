Hosea. Live the Message.

One of my favorite sayings is, “Who you are speaks so loud I can’t hear what you’re saying.” Isn’t that so true? Something inside us notices incongruity in people and immediately puts us on guard. My definition of integrity is simply ‘just being who you are.’ But that is not entirely correct. That would mean that if you were a habitual liar and someone confronted you about it, you could say, “but everyone knows I am a liar. I am a man of integrity. I never tell the truth.” But integrity is also linked to morality and God’s truth.

When I think of all the Prophets and great men of the Bible, they communicated exactly what the LORD told them to say. But there were at least 2 individuals who said more by what they did than by anything they ever said in words. They are Hosea, and Joseph the husband of Mary.

Let’s look at Hosea first. God commands him to take “a wife of whoredom” (Hosea 1:2). Right out of the gate Hosea is put into an extremely difficult situation. How did he respond? How would you respond? I think I would have said, “what was that again? I don’t think I heard you correctly. I was listening to music on Spotify. I thought I heard ‘whoredom.’ I know that can’t be right. Did you say for me to take a wife ‘from boredom’? Or maybe to take a wife that was ‘more dumb’? Or even take a wife ‘if I can afford ’em’?” Hosea says none of those things. He goes straight to the Mustang Ranch and finds a woman named Gomer (you can’t make this stuff up) and marries her. He then does the next thing God commanded him to do and that was to go on an impregnation spree. 3 children quickly show up in their nursery. But God is not done. He commands Hosea to give each child a weird name. He says name the first one, “God scatters,” and the next, “not loved,” and the third, “not My people.” I don’t know about you but those are 3 names I would never pick for my children. At a minimum I would have changed a few letters; She’s-real, Jo-mamma, and Jo-mammy. Not Hosea, he does exactly as the LORD directed him. Within one chapter Hosea is married to a prostitute and 3 children with stupid names. Surely at this point Hosea would provide some feedback to the LORD. Something like, “God, I see what you are doing here. You are making me and my wife of whoredom an object lesson for You and Your people. But wouldn’t it be easier just to tell them? I know it would be easier on me.”

But God had a very good reason, and He explains it in Hosea 1:4–5, 6–7 and 8–9, and then at length in Hosea 2. Perhaps Hosea breathed a sigh of relief. He might have thought, “Whew! Glad that’s over! Now I can get back to speaking God’s words. Maybe He’ll send Gomer back to the Mustang Ranch and get me remarried to a good Jewish woman. Build a house with a white picket fence. Homeschool the children.” But unfortunately, God is not done. There is more to be done.

He now tells him, “Your wife has left you. She has gone back to her whoring ways. Chasing men for anything they will give her for sex. Go and love your wife again, even though she commits adultery with other lovers. And yes, you will need to pay for her again. 15 pieces of silver, 5 bushels of barley and a measure of wine.” Again, I would have said something. “LORD, but she left on her own. She fled when I was asleep. She has made her bed; let her lie in it. And why do I have to give 3 years of money again? Is this going to be a recurring cost for the rest of my life? Aargh. You are Almighty God so I will do it but why do I have to ‘love her again’? Can’t she just sleep on the couch?” Nope. Hosea fully obeys (Hosea 3:1–3). He pays the $ and brings her back home. No more prostitution sex with her lovers. No more marriage sex with him. For many days. God gives a brief reason for all of this and finally has Hosea speak why he is doing this to all of Israel in Hosea 4:1–19.

God is the great communicator. He goes to incredible lengths to know and be known. He reveals Himself in creation, through His prophets, in dreams and visions, through His actions, through the holy scriptures, through the Holy Spirit and ultimately in Jesus Christ. But I left off something, someone very important. His people. You. You will be His witness! In your neighborhood, your city, your state, your country and to the ends of the earth (Acts 1:8). It’s not a decision of ‘if.’ For me personally Hosea is one of God’s most penetrating messages to my heart. What my sin looks like and feels like to God. Spiritual adultery. If Hosea just conveyed words about it I would have paid attention, but to see him live it as a microcosm of what God has endured for thousands of years breaks my heart. What if Hosea refused to do what God commanded him? What if he chose only to speak what God wanted as a compromise? God has chosen me and you to communicate His character, His heart, and His love to this world. We must do it His way. Actions speak louder than words. A picture is worth a thousand words. An image, a story, a decision… Every manner of communication must be obeyed. The infinite glory of God demands no less.

What Hosea actually did

ActionReferenceWhy it mattered
Married Gomer, daughter of Diblaim Hosea 1:2–3 God commanded Hosea to take “a wife of whoredom” (or a woman characterized by sexual unfaithfulness). Hosea went and married Gomer, portraying Israel’s unfaithfulness toward the LORD.
Had a son with Gomer Hosea 1:3 Gomer conceived and bore Hosea a son. The child’s name became part of the prophetic message.
Named his first son Jezreel Hosea 1:4–5 God instructed the name Jezreel, associated with coming judgment on the house of Jehu and the kingdom of Israel.
Named his daughter Lo-ruhamah Hosea 1:6–7 God commanded the name Lo-ruhamah, usually rendered “No Mercy,” “Not Pitied,” or “Not Loved,” signifying coming withdrawal of mercy from the northern kingdom.
Named his later son Lo-ammi Hosea 1:8–9 God commanded the name Lo-ammi, “Not My People,” expressing Israel’s broken covenant status.
Went again to love an adulterous woman Hosea 3:1 After the marital rupture portrayed in the book, God told Hosea, “Go again, love a woman” who was involved with another man — explicitly mirroring God’s continuing love for Israel despite its idolatry.
Bought / redeemed her Hosea 3:2 Hosea paid fifteen shekels of silver plus barley for her. The text reports the purchase, though it does not explicitly name the woman as Gomer in chapter 3; many readers identify her with Gomer.
Called her to exclusive, restored faithfulness Hosea 3:3 Hosea told her to remain with him “many days,” not to be sexually involved with another man; he pledged the same faithfulness. This enacted the future restoration and purification of Israel.
Spoke God’s message to Israel Hosea 1:1; 4–14 Hosea delivered God’s charges against Israel’s idolatry, injustice, false worship, political unfaithfulness, and lack of covenant knowledge, along with promises of judgment, healing, and restoration.

Next: the man who never says a word

Next, we will look at Joseph, the husband of Mary. He is probably the most overlooked saint in the Bible. Yet, in my opinion, he is one of the godliest. He should be celebrated and talked about much more than he is. Like Hosea, his actions speak louder than words. Nevertheless, since he never speaks — not one recorded word in all of Scripture — we tend to overlook him or sweep him under the carpet. That is Part 2, and it is coming.
