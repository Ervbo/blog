Biblical December 5, 2027

Which saint are you?

I'm not sure, but I think I've been completely absorbed by AI — remember the Borg from Star Trek? I recently stumbled across an AI site that will tell you if you're a saint or not. In fact, it'll tell you which saint you're most like.

Which saint are you?

You'll all be happy to hear that, despite no frontal lobe, I am still a saint. I don't mean to brag, but I'm most like Mother Teresa — the best of all the saints. I wonder which saint was the humblest. I'd be the best of that one too.

A few disclaimers. I'm not Catholic, but for me there's more doctrine that ties me to them than divides me — I actually use the Apostle's Creed as my litmus test for when I need to contend for the faith. Catholics believe all believers in Christ are saints, of course, but there's also an officially recognized list the church refers to as Canonized saints. Even though AI is convinced I'm Mother Teresa, I wanted to be Augustine — have you read his Confessions? — or maybe Aloysius Gonzaga, who lived his life with a great passion for youth, especially teenagers. Those of you who know me know that's exactly my thing. Also, I like Gonzaga every March Madness.

Obviously I'm joking about Mother Teresa being the best saint, and about being the best of the humblest one, but I was being truthful that the quiz landed on her for me. She really would be a good comparison if you looked hard into my 40-plus years of youth ministry. I always wanted to go lower, deeper, toward the most hurting, exploited, vile teenagers I could find. I'm not entirely sure why — I was a good kid, a good student, I had nothing in common with them except a terrible family life. I think that's what drove me deeper toward the hurting ones. But that was before my brain tumor. That was when I was a “good” saint.

After my brain tumor, I'm now the saint of pissing people off. St. Erv the Pisser Offer. Perhaps I've even touched your life in this way, and you've noticed how I stand out above the rest.

Hope you're all well. Let's press on to know the Lord (Hosea 6:3) and walk humbly with Him (Micah 6:8). — Erv
