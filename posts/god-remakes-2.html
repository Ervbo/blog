God REmakes. Part 2.

Part 2 of the God REmakes series.

In Part 1 I laid out the map: over 120 English words with the ‘re-’ prefix, every one of them describing something God is doing to what sin has damaged. Now we start walking the territory, a few words at a time. The format for each word: the definition, what to take to heart, and then the Scriptures — told simply, the way I would tell them to my grandkids. You will notice these first two words are a matched pair: both are about God’s people stopping, looking honestly at what has lapsed, and rebuilding it — and both live in the same era, the return from exile.

REEVALUATE

Definition
Revise or renew one’s assessment.

Take it to heart
God calls His people to examine their ways, test their hearts, and reconsider their priorities in light of His Word.

Haggai 1:5–7
God sent the prophet Haggai to speak to the leaders and people of Judah. The people had built nice homes for themselves, but they had left God’s temple — the place where they gathered to worship Him — in ruins. They kept saying, “We can rebuild God’s house later.” God told them to stop and think about what they were doing. They worked hard planting crops and earning money, but they never seemed to have enough. It was like putting money into a bag with holes: it kept disappearing. God explained that they had been so busy taking care of their own things that they had forgotten to honor Him. He told them to go get wood from the mountains and rebuild His house. The lesson is that God wants us to remember Him and put Him first, not only focus on our own wants and plans.

Lamentations 3:40
These verses say that when we do wrong things, we should not only complain about the trouble that follows. Instead, we should honestly look at our choices, admit where we have sinned, and turn back to God. It is like stopping when you realize you are walking the wrong way. We can pray to God with open hearts, tell Him we are sorry, and ask Him to help us follow Him again.

2 Corinthians 13:5
Paul says we should sometimes stop and check our hearts and actions. We can ask ourselves, “Do I trust Jesus? Am I trying to obey Him and love others?” It is not like a scary EKG. It is more like checking a flashlight to make sure its light is working. Jesus helps His followers live in a loving, honest, and kind way.

Psalm 139:23–24
This is a prayer asking God to look closely at our hearts. We can ask Him to help us notice worries, wrong choices, or unkind attitudes that we may not see ourselves. Then we ask God to forgive us, teach us what is right, and guide us on His good path that leads to life with Him forever.

REVAMP

Definition
To patch up or renovate; repair or restore.

Take it to heart
God renews what has become damaged or ineffective — in individual lives, in worship, and in community.

Nehemiah 8–10
The people of Jerusalem gathered to hear Ezra read God’s Word and explain what it meant. They realized they had not been obeying God, so they were sorry — but Nehemiah told them to celebrate too, because God’s joy could make them strong. They celebrated God’s special festival by making little leafy shelters, remembering how God took care of His people long ago. They ate together, shared food with people who needed it, and were happy because they understood God’s teaching. Then the people prayed and remembered God’s great kindness. They remembered that God made the world, rescued His people from Egypt, fed and led them in the desert, and kept forgiving them even when they were stubborn and disobeyed. Finally, the people said, “We want to obey God now.” Their leaders wrote down a serious promise to follow God’s instructions, care for His temple, give generously, and remember Him in their everyday lives. The big lesson is: listen to God’s Word, admit when you do wrong, thank God for His mercy, and choose to follow Him with joy.

Romans 12:2
Because God loves us and has done so much for us, we can give Him our whole lives — our hands, words, thoughts, and choices. That means trying to do what pleases Him every day, not just saying we love Him. We do not have to copy unkind or wrong things that other people do. Instead, we can ask God to help us think in new, good ways, so we can learn what He wants us to do.

Ephesians 4:22–24
When we learn about Jesus, He helps us leave behind old, wrong habits — like taking off dirty clothes — and choose a new way to live. We can ask God to help us have kind, honest, and loving thoughts and actions. These verses remind us to tell the truth and not let anger boss us around. If we get angry, we should calm down, talk kindly, and make things right as soon as we can instead of holding onto the anger.

Two words down. Do you see the pattern forming? Before God rebuilds anything — a temple, a wall, a heart — He first calls His people to look: reevaluate what has lapsed, then revamp what the years have broken. Next time we will take up more of the list. If a word on the map has already started preaching to you, tell me in the Comments — I may take requests.
