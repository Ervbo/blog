Keep the Words of This Prophecy

Brothers, blessed Sabbath to you all, and a blessed Resurrection Day tomorrow! I watched Dave's video this morning — here's my favorite one, even though it's a little dated: youtu.be.

I've seen a few emails floating around this week about the resurrection from Dewey and Dave — both great. I'm also still feeling the effects of Bill's email about the stones and how they'll refract God's pure light in heaven. You know, the Bible really is the greatest love story ever told. It might just be the greatest story, period — the plot, the twists, the drama, the emotion, the mystery, the characters, and then that perfect ending. It's staring us in the face in Revelation 22, and I honestly can't take it all in.

I've read the Bible cover to cover every year for 49.5 years now, and I'm as emotionally moved by it as I've ever been. I love what Bill C told me recently. I was a little stuck on one of our lesson questions — how does the New Jerusalem descending to earth affect you personally? That God dwells with man again, and the curse on man and creation is gone forever? Bill agreed it's too much to take in, then added: “but I enjoy trying.” That's really it, isn't it? We can't take it all in. Not yet, anyway. But what a joy in the trying.

I still can't wrap my head around what God has planned for me personally in heaven — and I don't just mean being with Him in complete love, purity, and intimacy, worshiping Him (I'm always afraid I write too much, but I have a lot to say there — maybe I'll share it in our last session). This time through Revelation I kept saying: we do a lot more than just worship Him in heaven. It's the Garden of Eden restored, on steroids. We get a life, an interaction with the new earth, with each other, and with God, that hasn't been experienced since the fall. What joy in that kind of perfection — we even judge angels! How do I even begin to wrap my head around that?

There I go again. I'm a walking rant when it comes to anything about God and His Word.

The real reason I'm writing is Lesson 29, Question 4: “In what ways are people blessed when they 'keep the words' of this prophecy, and how have you experienced this blessing?” We know what “keeping the words” means from the Old Testament (Deuteronomy 11:26–28) and from the Gospels (Matthew 7:24–25). But this question is asking specifically about the book of Revelation. I believe the answer is the same, but I wanted to reread the whole book with an eye for any nuance before I answered. Normally I don't mind doing that kind of thing — it takes time, but it's always worth it. I recall the first time I read through all four Gospels just tracking every verse with “one another” or “each other” in it. That took a while but might be the greatest thing I've ever found in Scripture. This time, since I was feeling impatient, I let AI help me instead.

So here's a cheat sheet of what to “keep” in Revelation — a minimum, really. If you want to take the time to reread the whole book and ask God to show you what words you need to hear, or re-hear, then I'd say do exactly that. As for what “the blessing” actually is, only you can answer that from your own experience. I can't wait to hear what it's been for each of you.
